\begin{abstract}
We study a concrete family of \emph{recursive knots}: starting from a round circle $K_0$, $K_d$ is the $(p_d,q_d)$ torus pattern on the tube of radius $r_d$ around $K_{d-1}$, placed with a rotation-minimising (Bishop) frame closed by a linear twist; the question is how much rope (length divided by thickness) such nested knots need. We prove that $K_d$ is embedded and smooth when $r_d<\thick(K_{d-1})$; that the closed frame has linking number $\Wr K_{d-1}-\alpha_d/2\pi$ with its core, so $K_d$ is an explicit cable of $K_{d-1}$; two-sided length bounds; and, for $p_d=2$, $\thick(K_d)\le r_d$, so $\Rop(K_d)\ge L(K_d)/r_d$. For the lower bound $\thick(K_d)\ge\min(\thick K_{d-1}-r_d,\ c\,r_d\sin(\pi/p_d))$ with $p_d=2$, $r_d\le\thick(K_{d-1})/2$: in the default family $(2,3)$ every doubly critical pair of $K_d$ with distinct base points is at distance $\ge\min(2(\thick K_{d-1}-r_d),\,r_d)$ at every depth, so the bound with $c=1/2$ reduces to $\minRad(K_d)\ge r_d/2$, which we prove at depth one ($\thick(K_1)\ge r_1/2$ for every $f=r_1\le1/2$). For $d\ge2$ it follows from a bound $(\mathrm H_3)$ on derivatives of the speed and of the curvature vector of $K_{d-1}$, and fails for an arbitrary base without one. At $d=2$ we prove it by a computer-assisted argument (interval arithmetic on the explicit base $K_1$: $\minRad(K_2)\ge\DtwoCertMinRad\,r_2$ for every $f\le1/2$ and every choice of phases), so $\thick(K_d)\ge r_d/2$ holds for $d\le2$. We show that the speed and curvature of $K_d$ are controlled by the $(\mathrm H_3)$ data of $K_{d-1}$ and that for $d\ge3$ a growth condition on these data suffices, but that $\mu(K_d)$, the size of the derivative of the curvature vector, cannot be bounded by any locally bounded function of the $(\mathrm H_3)$ data of an arbitrary base (one derivative is lost per depth in this sense; in the family $\mu$ grows slowly on the computed levels). $(\mathrm H_3)$ uniformly in $d$, hence the bound for $d\ge3$, remains open, although every condition holds on the polygons (grid evaluation, uncertified). Under the thickness bound (proved for $d\le2$), $\Rop_d\le2\pi\Lambda_{\Rop}^d$ with an explicit $\Lambda_{\Rop}$. Explicit polygons with up to $\NvHalfThree$ vertices give $L/\tau=\RopHalfOne$, $\RopHalfTwo$, $\RopHalfThree$ at depths $1,2,3$ for $(2,3)$ and $f=1/2$, stable to $\ConvMaxRelRop\%$ under doubling of the resolution: numerically verified upper bounds for explicit polygons, not certified smooth thicknesses. No stationarity, optimality or recursive flow law is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and status}

\paragraph{The research line.} Line RTK001 (``Geometry of recursive knots'') asks how nested knots---knots tied on the tube of other knots, recursively---can be represented as tubes without self-intersection, and how much length that costs. Its public record states one finding (a feasible upper bound at depth one) and an explicit scope restriction: neither stationarity nor optimality of any configuration is certified, and no general recursive flow law has been established. No manuscript of the line existed before this draft; the family below was fixed from the record. Four internal reviews have been applied (v0.2--v0.5, 30/09--03/10/2026): the thickness hypothesis of v0.1 was false for an arbitrary base (Remark~\ref{rem:Hcfalse}); every constant is labelled as analytic or as a grid evaluation; the same-strand case is proved (Proposition~\ref{prop:samestrand}); the frame normalisation, on which the margins of Conjecture~\ref{conj} depend, is fixed in Definition~\ref{def:family}; a partial result on the uniformity of $(\mathrm H_3)$ is added (Lemma~\ref{lem:speedrec}--Proposition~\ref{prop:noclose}); and v0.5 adds a computer-assisted proof of the case $d=2$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:d2cert}), a joint scan of the two frame normalisations that affect $K_3$, and states Conjecture~\ref{conj} uniformly in the phases.

\paragraph{What is done here.} We (i) fix a definite family of recursive knots (Section~\ref{sec:setting}); (ii) prove embeddedness, the framing used, two-sided length bounds, an upper bound on the thickness and most of the lower bound, isolating what is not proved in one hypothesis on the family (Section~\ref{sec:theory}); (iii) compute explicit polygonal configurations at depths $1$ to $3$, their polygonal thickness and ropelength, with a resolution check (Section~\ref{sec:exp}); (iv) tabulate every claim with its status (Section~\ref{sec:claims}). Lower bounds for general knots are only quoted from the literature (Remark~\ref{rem:lower}); the only lower bound we derive is internal to the family (Proposition~\ref{prop:upper}).

\section{Setting}\label{sec:setting}

\subsection{Thickness and ropelength}

Let $K\subset\R^3$ be a closed $C^{1,1}$ curve of length $L(K)$. Its \emph{thickness} $\thick(K)$ is the supremum of $\rho$ such that the open tube of radius $\rho$ about $K$ is embedded (normal injectivity radius). Two equivalent descriptions are classical. Litherland, Simon, Durumeric and Rawdon \cite{LSDR1999} show, for $C^2$ curves (extended to the $C^{1,1}$ case in \cite{CKS2002}; all curves $K_d$ below are $C^\infty$),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lsdr}
\thick(K)=\min\bigl(\minRad(K),\ \tfrac12\dcsd(K)\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $\minRad$ is the minimal radius of curvature and $\dcsd$ the \emph{doubly critical self distance}, the minimal distance between pairs $x\neq y$ of $K$ whose chord is perpendicular to $K$ at both ends. Gonzalez and Maddocks \cite{GonzalezMaddocks1999} show $\thick(K)=\inf_{x\neq y}\rho_{pt}(x,y)$, with $\rho_{pt}(x,y)=|x-y|/(2\sin\angle(T_x,\,y-x))$ the radius of the circle through $y$ tangent to $K$ at $x$. The \emph{ropelength} is $\Rop(K)=L(K)/\thick(K)$; it is scale invariant, $\Rop(K)\ge 2\pi$ for every closed curve (Fenchel's theorem \cite{Fenchel1929}, total curvature $\ge2\pi$, together with $\kappa\le1/\thick$), with equality only for the circle, and $\thick(K)\le\minRad(K)$ always.

\subsection{Frames, holonomy and the closing twist}

Let $K$ be a closed $C^2$ regular curve parametrised on $[0,2\pi)$ (not necessarily by arclength), with unit tangent $T$ and curvature $\kappa$. A \emph{rotation-minimising} or Bishop frame \cite{Bishop1975} is an orthonormal frame $(T,N_0,B_0)$ with $N_0'=-\kappa_1|K'|\,T$, $B_0'=-\kappa_2|K'|\,T$, $\kappa_1^2+\kappa_2^2=\kappa^2$; it is unique up to a constant rotation in the normal plane (fixed in Definition~\ref{def:family}) and is in general \emph{not} periodic: after one turn the transported normal is rotated by a holonomy angle $\alpha\in(-\pi,\pi]$. We close it by the linear twist $\omega(t)=-\alpha t/2\pi$, i.e.\ $N=\cos\omega\,N_0+\sin\omega\,B_0$, $B=T\times N$. The closed frame satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:frame}
N'=-\tilde\kappa_1|K'|\,T+\omega' B,\qquad B'=-\tilde\kappa_2|K'|\,T-\omega' N,\qquad |\omega'|=\frac{|\alpha|}{2\pi}\le\frac12 ,
\end{equation}
with the rotated curvature components $\tilde\kappa_1=\kappa_1\cos\omega+\kappa_2\sin\omega$, $\tilde\kappa_2=-\kappa_1\sin\omega+\kappa_2\cos\omega$ (same norm $\kappa$); below we write $\kappa_1,\kappa_2$ for whichever frame is in use.

\subsection{The family of recursive knots}\label{sec:family}

\begin{definition}[iterated cable by frame offsets]\label{def:family}
Let $K_0$ be the circle of radius $R_0=1$. For $d\ge1$ choose coprime integers $p_d\ge2$, $q_d\ge1$, a radius $r_d>0$ and a phase $\varphi_d$. Reparametrise $K_{d-1}$ to period $2\pi$ by the linear change $t\mapsto t/p_{d-1}$ (so that $K_{d-1}$ is traversed at parameter speed $|K_{d-1}'|$, not at unit speed), let $(N_{d-1},B_{d-1})$ be its closed Bishop frame, normalised by $N_{d-1}(0)=$ the unit projection of $e_z$ onto the normal plane at $K_{d-1}(0)$ (of $e_x$ if $|T(0)\cdot e_z|>0.9$), and set for $t\in[0,2\pi p_d)$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Kd}
K_d(t)=K_{d-1}(t\bmod 2\pi)+r_d\bigl[\cos\theta_d(t)\,N_{d-1}(t)+\sin\theta_d(t)\,B_{d-1}(t)\bigr],\qquad \theta_d(t)=\frac{q_d}{p_d}\,t+\varphi_d .
\end{equation}
Rotating $N_{d-1}(0)$ by an angle $\vartheta$ amounts to replacing $\varphi_d$ by $\varphi_d+\vartheta$, so a choice of normalisation is a choice of phases; for $p_d=2$, $q_d$ odd, $\varphi_d+\pi$ gives the same curve. The default is $(p_d,q_d)=(2,3)$, $\varphi_d=0$ at every depth; $r_d$ is a fraction $f$ of the thickness of $K_{d-1}$ (the guaranteed lower bound $\rho_{d-1}$ in the theory, the measured polygonal value in the experiments).
\end{definition}

$K_1$ is the $(p_1,q_1)$ torus knot on the round torus of radii $(1,r_1)$; the knot type of $K_d$ for $d\ge2$ depends on the framing, which Lemma~\ref{lem:frame} makes explicit. All results of Section~\ref{sec:theory} hold for every choice of $N_{d-1}(0)$ and $\varphi_d$; lengths and thicknesses of the computed levels do not depend on it at the printed precision, but the margins of Conjecture~\ref{conj} do (Section~\ref{sec:exp}). The pattern angle advances in the parameter $t$, not in arclength; since the parametrisation of $K_{d-1}$ is the one inherited from the construction, the \emph{pitch} of the strands relative to the base (the quantity $h$ of Section~\ref{sec:theory}) is a property of the family, not a free parameter.

\subsection{Polygonal measurements}\label{sec:poly}

Numerically a curve is a closed polygon $P=(x_0,\dots,x_{N-1})$. We use $L(P)=\sum|x_{i+1}-x_i|$; $\minRad(P)$, the minimum circumradius of consecutive triples; $\dcsd(P)$, the minimal distance $|x_i-x_j|$ over \emph{doubly critical vertex pairs}, i.e.\ pairs where $|x_i-x_j|$ is a local minimum in $i$ and in $j$ (the diagonal $D_{ii}=0$ is kept in the neighbourhood test, which excludes near-diagonal pairs); and the proxy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tau}
\tau(P)=\min\bigl(\minRad(P),\ \tfrac12\dcsd(P)\bigr),\qquad \Rop(P)=L(P)/\tau(P).
\end{equation}
The local-minimum test could miss doubly critical pairs of saddle type; as a safeguard we also compute $\tau_{pt}(P)=\min_{i\ne j}|x_j-x_i|^2/(2|T_i\times(x_j-x_i)|)$ with central-difference tangents $T_i$, which uses no criticality test. Neither $\tau$ nor $\tau_{pt}$ is a certified thickness of a smooth curve (for the thickness of polygons themselves see \cite{Rawdon2003}); they are the quantities of \eqref{eq:lsdr} evaluated on an explicit polygon, and their stability under doubling of $N$ is the main evidence offered that they approximate the smooth values. Vertex pairs may overestimate the polygon--polygon distance; the minimal segment--segment distance over pairs of edges with cyclic index separation $>N/10$, divided by the vertex $\dcsd$, is $\SegRatioQuarterOne$, $\SegRatioHalfOne$ ($d=1$, $f=0.25,0.5$) and $\SegRatioHalfTwo$ ($d=2$, $f=0.5$), and near the critical pair the ratio is $\ge\SegLocalMin$ in every level examined (\texttt{experiments/aux\_checks.py}). Writhe is the Gauss double sum over pairs of edges (midpoint rule); its accuracy is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:exp}.

\section{Results}\label{sec:theory}

Throughout, $K=K_{d-1}$ is a closed $C^\infty$ regular curve of thickness $\tau=\thick(K)>0$, hence of maximal curvature $\kappa_{\max}\le1/\tau$, carrying the parametrisation of period $2\pi$ inherited from Definition~\ref{def:family}, with speed $v=|K'|\in[v_{\min},v_{\max}]$; $K_d$ is built by \eqref{eq:Kd} with $r=r_d$, $p=p_d$, $q=q_d$, and $f=r/\tau$.

\begin{lemma}[embedding]\label{lem:embed}\status{proved here}
If $r<\tau$ then $K_d$ is a regular $C^\infty$ closed curve, injective on $[0,2\pi p)$, contained in the torus $\partial\{x:\operatorname{dist}(x,K)<r\}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Smoothness: the Bishop frame of a $C^\infty$ curve is $C^\infty$ (it solves a linear ODE with smooth coefficients), and \eqref{eq:Kd} is a smooth expression in it; periodicity of $K_d$ in $t$ with period $2\pi p$ holds because $\theta_d(t+2\pi p)=\theta_d(t)+2\pi q$ and the closed frame has period $2\pi$. Regularity: differentiating \eqref{eq:Kd} with \eqref{eq:frame},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deriv}
K_d'=|K'|\bigl(1-r(\kappa_1\cos\theta+\kappa_2\sin\theta)\bigr)\,T+r(\theta'+\omega')(-\sin\theta\,N+\cos\theta\,B),
\end{equation}
and $|\kappa_1\cos\theta+\kappa_2\sin\theta|\le\kappa\le1/\tau$, so the tangential coefficient is at least $|K'|(1-r/\tau)>0$. Injectivity: let $t_1\neq t_2$ in $[0,2\pi p)$. If $t_1\not\equiv t_2\pmod{2\pi}$, the two points lie in the normal discs of radius $r<\tau$ at distinct points of $K$; by definition of thickness these discs are disjoint, so the points differ. If $t_1\equiv t_2\pmod{2\pi}$, then $t_1-t_2=2\pi k$ with $0<|k|<p$ and $\theta_d(t_1)-\theta_d(t_2)=2\pi qk/p\notin2\pi\Z$ because $\gcd(p,q)=1$; the two points lie in the same normal disc at the same radius $r>0$ but at different angles, hence differ. Containment in the torus is immediate from \eqref{eq:Kd}.
\end{proof}

The constant $1/2$ in $r\le\tau/2$, used from Lemma~\ref{lem:length} on, is what the estimates below need; embeddedness only needs $r<\tau$.

\begin{lemma}[the framing used, and the knot type]\label{lem:frame}\status{proved here, from classical results}
Let $\alpha\in(-\pi,\pi]$ be the holonomy of the Bishop frame of $K$. Then the closed frame $(N,B)$ is a framing of $K$ with linking number $n:=\Lk=\Wr K-\alpha/2\pi\in\Z$; in particular $\alpha\equiv2\pi\Wr K\pmod{2\pi}$, and $n=\operatorname{round}(\Wr K)$ whenever $\Wr K\notin\Z+\tfrac12$ (if $\Wr K\in\Z+\frac12$ then $\alpha=\pi$ and $n=\Wr K-\frac12$). With the convention that a $(p,q')$-cable of $K$ winds $p$ times along $K$ and $q'$ times around it relative to a framing of linking number $0$ (the Seifert framing), $K_d$ is the $(p,\,q+pn)$-cable of $K$ \cite{Lickorish1997}. In particular $K_1$ is the torus knot $T(p_1,q_1)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By the C\u{a}lug\u{a}reanu--White--Fuller formula \cite{Calugareanu1961,White1969,Fuller1971}, $\Lk=\operatorname{Tw}+\Wr$ for any framing. The Bishop frame has zero twist density; the closing rotation $\omega$ adds total twist $\operatorname{Tw}=\frac1{2\pi}\int\omega'\,dt=-\alpha/2\pi\in[-\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$. Hence $\Lk=\Wr-\alpha/2\pi$, and since $\Lk\in\Z$ the congruence and the rounding rule follow. The pattern \eqref{eq:Kd} winds $q$ times around the meridian relative to the frame $(N,B)$ while going $p$ times along $K$; relative to a framing of linking number $0$ it winds $q+pn$ times, which is the stated cable. For $K_0$ a planar circle, $\Wr=0=\alpha$ and $K_1$ is the standard torus knot.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[length, two-sided]\label{lem:length}\status{proved here}
If $r\le\tau/2$ then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:length}
p\,L(K)\,(1-r\kappa_{\max})\ \le\ L(K_d)\ \le\ p\,L(K)\,(1+r\kappa_{\max})+r\,(2\pi q+p|\alpha|)\ \le\ p\,L(K)\,(1+r/\tau)+r\,(2\pi q+\pi p).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
From \eqref{eq:deriv} and the triangle inequality, $|K_d'|\le|K'|(1+r\kappa)+r(|\theta'|+|\omega'|)=|K'|(1+r\kappa)+r(q/p+|\alpha|/2\pi)$. Integrate over $[0,2\pi p)$: the first term gives $p\int_0^{2\pi}|K'|(1+r\kappa)\,dt\le pL(K)(1+r\kappa_{\max})$, the second $r(2\pi q+p|\alpha|)$. Then use $\kappa_{\max}\le1/\tau$ and $|\alpha|\le\pi$. For the lower bound, $|K_d'|$ is at least its tangential component $|K'|(1-r\kappa)\ge|K'|(1-r\kappa_{\max})$; integrate.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The term $p|\alpha|r\le\pi pr$ from the closing twist, absent from a naive count ``$p$ copies plus $q$ meridians'', is needed in the proof because the Bishop frame of a non-planar $K$ does not close; numerically the bound without it also holds in all $\AllLevels$ levels (minimal ratio bound$/L$ $\NoTwistMinRatio$).
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[upper bound on the thickness and a lower bound on the ropelength, $p=2$]\label{prop:upper}\status{proved here}
Let $p=2$ ($q$ odd) and $r<\tau$. Then $\thick(K_d)\le r$, hence $\Rop(K_d)\ge L(K_d)/r\ge pL(K)(1-r\kappa_{\max})/r$. In the family with $r_d=f\,\thick(K_{d-1})$ and $f\le1/2$ this gives $\Rop(K_d)\ge\frac{p(1-f)}{f}\Rop(K_{d-1})$, so $\Rop(K_d)\ge2\pi\bigl(p(1-f)/f\bigr)^d$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The two points $K_d(t)$ and $K_d(t+2\pi)$ lie in the same normal disc of $K$ at antipodal angles ($\theta$ and $\theta+\pi q$, $q$ odd), so their chord is $-2r(\cos\theta\,N+\sin\theta\,B)$, orthogonal to $T$ and to $-\sin\theta\,N+\cos\theta\,B$, hence by \eqref{eq:deriv} orthogonal to $K_d'$ at both ends: the pair is doubly critical at distance $2r$, so $\dcsd(K_d)\le2r$ and $\thick(K_d)\le r$ by \eqref{eq:lsdr} (equivalently, $\rho_{pt}=r$ for this pair). The rest is Lemma~\ref{lem:length} with $\kappa_{\max}\le1/\tau$, $r=f\tau$ and $\Rop(K_{d-1})=L(K_{d-1})/\tau$.
\end{proof}

For the default family at $f=1/2$ the internal lower bound is $2\pi\cdot2^d$, i.e.\ $\LowerAprioriHalfOne$, $\LowerAprioriHalfTwo$, $\LowerAprioriHalfThree$ at $d=1,2,3$, against the measured $\RopHalfOne$, $\RopHalfTwo$, $\RopHalfThree$; with the measured $L(K_d)$ the bound $L(K_d)/r_d$ is attained whenever $\thick(K_d)=r_d$ (Section~\ref{sec:exp}). The lower bound of \eqref{eq:length} holds in all $\AllLevels$ measured levels ($\AllLenLowerOk$ of $\AllLevels$).

\paragraph{Thickness of $K_d$: what is proved and what is not.} Write $s_p=2r\sin(\pi/p)$ for the chord between adjacent strands in a normal disc of $K$ (the $p$ points $K_d(t+2\pi k)$, $k=0,\dots,p-1$, sit at the $p$ equally spaced angles $\theta+2\pi qk/p$, since $\gcd(p,q)=1$).

\begin{proposition}[two of the three cases]\label{prop:partial}\status{proved here}
If $r\le\tau/2$: (a) two points of $K_d$ in the same normal disc of $K$ are at distance $\ge s_p$; (b) two points of $K_d$ whose base points on $K$ are at distance $\ge2\tau$ are at distance $\ge2(\tau-r)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a) They are at the same radius $r$ and at angles differing by $2\pi qk/p$, $0<k<p$, so their distance is $2r|\sin(\pi qk/p)|\ge2r\sin(\pi/p)=s_p$ because $qk\not\equiv0\pmod p$. (b) Triangle inequality: $|K_d(t_1)-K_d(t_2)|\ge|K(t_1)-K(t_2)|-2r$.
\end{proof}

Not covered by Proposition~\ref{prop:partial} are the pairs with $0<|K(t_1)-K(t_2)|<2\tau$, and the curvature of $K_d$. These include nearby base points (the helix geometry of $p$ strands on a bent tube) and base points on different arcs of $K$, e.g.\ across the hole of a torus: at $f=1/2$, $d=1$ the doubly critical vertex pair that realises $\dcsd$ has base points $\CritSepHalf^\circ$ apart on $K_0$, at distance $\CritBaseDistHalf<2\tau_0=2$ (\texttt{aux\_checks.py}), while for $f\le0.35$ it is the antipodal pair of one normal disc. For a straight tube the $p$ strands are helices with pitch parameter $h=|K'|p/q$ and the antipodal pair ($p=2$) is a local minimum of the strand distance exactly when $h\ge r$; at depth one the relevant pitch on the inner equator is $(1-r)p/q$, and $(1-r)p/q\ge r$ iff $r\le p/(p+q)=0.4$ for $(2,3)$, consistent with $\thick(K_1)=r_1$ for $f\le0.35$ and $\thick(K_1)<r_1$ for $f=0.5$.

\paragraph{Notation for the local case.} Let $(T,N_0,B_0)$ be the (non-periodic) Bishop frame of $K$ and $\alpha$ its holonomy, and set
\[
m:=\frac qp-\frac{\alpha}{2\pi},\qquad h_{\min}:=\frac{v_{\min}}{m},\qquad
\Theta_{\min}:=\frac{h_{\min}}{\tau},\qquad \zeta_{\min}:=\frac{(1-f)\,h_{\min}}{r},\qquad f=\frac r\tau\le\frac12 .
\]
In the parallel frame the strand of \eqref{eq:Kd} turns at the constant rate $m$ (its angle is $\psi=\theta+\omega=mt+\varphi$), so $h=v/m$ is the pitch parameter above ($|K'|p/q$ when $\alpha=0$) and $\Theta_{\min}$ is the smallest pitch in units of the base thickness. For two points $X_i=K(t_i)+rU_i$ of $K_d$ with distinct base points, let $\sigma$ be the length of the \emph{shorter} base arc between $K(t_1)$ and $K(t_2)$, $\delta=\sigma/\tau$, $s$ the base-parameter difference along that arc and $u=m|s|$ the relative turning of the strands along it. The integer $k=(t_2-t_1-s)/2\pi\bmod p$ is the strand index of $X_2$ relative to $X_1$ \emph{along that arc} (for $p=2$ the two strands are exchanged by one turn around $K$, so ``same strand'' is meaningful only relative to a base arc). With $\ell_0(\delta)=2\tau\sin(\delta/2)$, $E(\delta)=\tau(1-\cos\delta)$ for $\delta\le\pi/2$ and $E(\delta)=\tau(1+\delta-\pi/2)$ for $\delta>\pi/2$, $\beta(\delta)=\delta^2/(4\sin(\delta/2))$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:HcLP}
\Lambda(\delta)=\ell_0-\frac{2rE}{\ell_0},\qquad
P_1(\delta)=\Bigl[\,2\cos\!\Bigl(\tfrac{\delta}{2\Theta_{\min}}\Bigr)\sqrt{1-\beta(\delta)^2}-\tfrac{\delta^2}{3}\Bigr]_+
\end{equation}
(with $P_1:=0$ if $\delta>\pi\Theta_{\min}$ or $\beta>1$), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hcc1}
c_1(f,\Theta_{\min}):=\frac1{2r}\,\inf_{0<\delta<\pi}\ \max\Bigl\{\sqrt{\Lambda(\delta)_+^2+r^2P_1(\delta)^2},\ \ell_0(\delta)-2r\Bigr\}.
\end{equation}
All lengths in \eqref{eq:Hcc1} scale with $r$, so $c_1$ depends only on $(f,\Theta_{\min})$; $\ell_0/r-2$ and $\Lambda/r=(2/f)\sin(\delta/2)-(E/\tau)/\sin(\delta/2)$ decrease in $f$ and $P_1$ does not depend on $f$ and increases in $\Theta_{\min}$, so $c_1$ is non-increasing in $f$ and non-decreasing in $\Theta_{\min}$.

\begin{lemma}[pairs on different strands, $p=2$]\label{lem:Hc1}\status{proved here}
Let $p=2$, $q$ odd, $r\le\tau/2$. Any two points $X_1,X_2$ of $K_d$ with distinct base points and strand index $k=1$ relative to the shorter base arc satisfy
\[
|X_2-X_1|\ \ge\ \min\bigl(2(\tau-r),\ 2\,c_1(f,\Theta_{\min})\,r\bigr).
\]
Moreover $c_1(\tfrac12,\tfrac23)\ge\tfrac12$ (a grid evaluation gives $\HcConeBase$), hence $c_1\ge\tfrac12$ whenever $f\le\frac12$ and $\Theta_{\min}\ge\frac23$. For the default family $(p,q)=(2,3)$ with $r_j\le\thick(K_{j-1})/2$ for all $j\le d$ one has $\Theta_{\min}\ge\tfrac23$ at every depth, so every such pair is at distance $\ge\min(2(\tau-r),\,r)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1 (chord of a thick curve).} Let $x_0=K(0)$ and $g(\sigma)=|K(\sigma)-x_0|$ in arclength. A local minimum $y_0$ of $g$ in $(0,L)$ has $(y_0-x_0)\perp T(y_0)$, hence $\rho_{pt}(y_0,x_0)=|x_0-y_0|/2\ge\tau$, so $|x_0-y_0|\ge2\tau$. Consequently $\{g<2\tau\}$ consists of an initial interval $[0,a)$ and a final one $(L-b,L)$ on which $g$ is monotone (a non-monotone stretch below $2\tau$ would contain an interior local minimum below $2\tau$), and on them $g'=\cos\angle(T,K-x_0)\ge\sqrt{1-g^2/4\tau^2}$ a.e.\ (from $\rho_{pt}(K(\sigma),x_0)\ge\tau$), which integrates to $g(\sigma)\ge2\tau\sin(\sigma/2\tau)$ and shows $a,b\le\pi\tau$ (this is the classical chord--arc inequality for thick curves, cf.\ \cite{GonzalezMaddocks1999,CKS2002}). Hence for base points with shorter arc $\sigma=\tau\delta\le L/2$: either $\delta\ge\pi$ and $|x_0-y_0|\ge2\tau$ (then Proposition~\ref{prop:partial}(b) gives $|X_2-X_1|\ge2(\tau-r)$), or $\delta<\pi$ and $\ell:=|x_0-y_0|\ge\ell_0(\delta)$.

\emph{Step 2 (elementary estimates, $\delta<\pi$).} Let $x_0=K(t_1)$, $y_0=K(t_2)$, $e=(y_0-x_0)/\ell$ and $\bar T=\sigma^{-1}\int vT\,dt$ over the arc, so $e=\bar T/|\bar T|$, $|\bar T|=\ell/\sigma$. Since $|dT/d\sigma|=\kappa\le1/\tau$: (E2) $|\bar T-T(x_0)|\le\delta/2$; (E3) for $w\perp T(x_0)$, $|w\cdot e|=|w\cdot\Pi e|\le|w|\,|\bar T-T(x_0)|\,\sigma/\ell\le|w|\beta(\delta)$, where $\Pi$ is the projection onto the normal plane at $x_0$; (E4) for unit $w\perp T(x_0)$, $|w\cdot(y_0-x_0)|=|\int v\,w\cdot\Pi T\,dt|\le\int_0^\sigma\sin\min(\sigma'/\tau,\pi/2)\,d\sigma'=E(\delta)$, and likewise for $w\perp T(y_0)$; (E5) if $\tilde U_2$ denotes the vector with the same parallel-frame angle as $U_2$ but in the normal plane at $x_0$, then $U_2-\tilde U_2=-\int\kappa_\perp vT\,dt$ with $|\kappa_\perp|\le\kappa$ (differentiate $\cos\psi_2N_0+\sin\psi_2B_0$ along the arc), so $|U_2-\tilde U_2|\le\delta$ and its component orthogonal to $e$ is at most $\int\kappa v\,|T-\bar T|\,dt\le\int_0^\sigma\frac{\sigma'^2+(\sigma-\sigma')^2}{2\sigma\tau^2}\,d\sigma'=\delta^2/3$; (E6) $\tilde U_2-U_1$ lies in the normal plane at $x_0$ and has length $2|\sin(\Delta\psi/2)|$, where $\Delta\psi=ms+2\pi qk/p$ is the relative angle of the strands after parallel transport along the arc (the holonomy terms cancel because $N_0(t+2\pi)$ is $N_0(t)$ rotated by $\alpha$ and $m$ contains $-\alpha/2\pi$), i.e.\ $|\sin(\Delta\psi/2)|=|\cos(u/2)|$ for $k=1$ and $p=2$.

\emph{Step 3 (decomposition).} Write $X_2-X_1=(y_0-x_0)+r(U_2-U_1)=A_ee+Q$ with $Q\perp e$. By (E4), $A_e=\ell+r(U_2-U_1)\cdot e\ge\ell-2rE/\ell\ge\Lambda(\delta)$, since $\ell\mapsto\ell-2rE/\ell$ is increasing and $\ell\ge\ell_0$. By (E3), (E5), (E6), $|Q|=r|(U_2-U_1)_{\perp e}|\ge r\bigl[2|\cos(u/2)|\sqrt{1-\beta^2}-\delta^2/3\bigr]_+$. Finally $u=m|s|\le m\sigma/v_{\min}=\delta/\Theta_{\min}$ and $|\cos(u/2)|$ is decreasing on $[0,\pi]$, so $|\cos(u/2)|\ge\cos(\delta/2\Theta_{\min})$ when $\delta\le\pi\Theta_{\min}$; together with the trivial bound $|X_2-X_1|\ge\ell-2r\ge\ell_0-2r$ this gives $|X_2-X_1|\ge2c_1(f,\Theta_{\min})r$ for every pair with $\delta<\pi$.

\emph{Step 4 ($c_1(\frac12,\frac23)\ge\frac12$).} Put $r=1$, $\tau=2$, $\Theta_{\min}=2/3$; we show that the maximum in \eqref{eq:Hcc1} is $\ge1$ for every $\delta\in(0,\pi)$. For $\delta\in[\pi/3,\pi/2]$, $\Lambda=2(\tau-r)\sin(\delta/2)=2\sin(\delta/2)\ge1$. For $\delta\in[\pi/2,\pi]$, with $\varsigma=\sin(\delta/2)\in[1/\sqrt2,1]$, $\Lambda=4\varsigma-(1+2\arcsin\varsigma-\pi/2)/\varsigma$, and $\Lambda\ge1$ amounts to $\phi(\varsigma):=4\varsigma^2-\varsigma-1+\pi/2-2\arcsin\varsigma\ge0$; $\phi''=8-2\varsigma(1-\varsigma^2)^{-3/2}$ changes sign once on the interval, so $\phi'$ is increasing then decreasing, and since $\phi'(1/\sqrt2)>0$ and $\phi'(1^-)=-\infty$, $\phi$ increases then decreases; its minimum is at an endpoint, $\phi(1/\sqrt2)=0.29$, $\phi(1)=0.43$. For $\delta\in(0,\pi/3)$ we need $4\sin^2(\delta/2)+P_1^2\ge1$. On $[0.6,\pi/3]$: $4\sin^2(\delta/2)\ge4\sin^2(0.3)>0.349$, $\cos(3\delta/4)\ge\cos(\pi/4)>0.7071$, $\beta\le\beta(\pi/3)<0.5484$ ($\beta$ is increasing), so $\sqrt{1-\beta^2}>0.8362$, and $\delta^2/3\le0.3656$; hence $P_1>2\cdot0.7071\cdot0.8362-0.3656>0.8169$, $P_1^2>0.667$, and the sum exceeds $1.01$. On $(0,0.6]$: $\cos(3\delta/4)\ge\cos(0.45)\ge0.900$, $\beta\le\beta(0.6)\le0.305$, $\delta^2/3\le0.12$, so $P_1\ge2\cdot0.900\cdot0.952-0.12\ge1.59$. All inequalities are non-strict where needed: at $\delta=\pi/3$, $\Lambda=1$ exactly. Hence $c_1(\frac12,\frac23)\ge\frac12$. The value $\HcConeBase$ (attained near $\delta=1.23$; an independent evaluation during the review agreed to four digits) is a grid infimum with a Lipschitz constant estimated on the grid itself (\texttt{experiments/check\_Hc.py}), not a certified bound.

\emph{Step 5 (the family).} $\Theta_{\min}=v_{\min}/(m\tau)$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:upper}, $\tau_{d-1}=\thick(K_{d-1})\le r_{d-1}\le\tau_{d-2}/2$, so $\tau_{d-1}\le2^{-(d-1)}$. After the reparametrisation $t\mapsto t/p$, \eqref{eq:deriv} gives $v_{d,\min}\ge p(1-f)v_{d-1,\min}\ge v_{d-1,\min}$ for $p=2$, $f\le1/2$, so $v_{\min}\ge v_0=1$ at every depth; and $m\le q/p+1/2=2$. Hence $\Theta_{\min}\ge2^{d-2}\ge1$ for $d\ge2$, while for $d=1$ ($\alpha=0$, $m=3/2$, $v=\tau=1$) $\Theta_{\min}=2/3$. Monotonicity of $c_1$ concludes.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[curvature of $K_d$]\label{prop:Hccurv}\status{proved here, under $(\mathrm H_3)$}
Assume in addition $(\mathrm H_3)$: $\sup|v'|\le M_v$ and $\sup\sqrt{\kappa_1'^2+\kappa_2'^2}\le M_\kappa$ for $K$ (derivatives in $t$, parallel-frame curvatures; $\kappa_1'^2+\kappa_2'^2=\kappa'^2+v^2\kappa^2\tau_{\rm tors}^2$ includes the torsion). Then, with $g(\zeta)=\zeta(\zeta^2+2)/(\zeta^2+1)^{3/2}$ and $G(\zeta_{\min})=\sup_{\zeta\ge\zeta_{\min}}g\le g(\sqrt2)<\GMax$: (a)
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hckappa}
\kappa_{K_d}\ \le\ \bar\kappa_d:=\frac{G(\zeta_{\min})}{\tau-r}+\frac1{r(1+\zeta_{\min}^2)}
+\frac{rm\,[M_v(1+f)+r\,v_{\max}M_\kappa]}{\bigl(v_{\min}^2(1-f)^2+r^2m^2\bigr)^{3/2}},
\qquad \minRad(K_d)\ge1/\bar\kappa_d ;
\end{equation}
(b) $\minRad(K_d)\ge1/\hat\kappa_d$, with $A=v^2(1-x)^2$, $B=r^2m^2$, $b_U=v^2(1-x)x/r-rm^2$ and
\[
\hat\kappa_d:=\sup_{|x|\le f,\ v_{\min}\le v\le v_{\max}}\frac{\bigl[b_U^2(A+B)+\bigl(rm(M_v(1-x)+rvM_\kappa)+v(A+2B)\sqrt{\tau^{-2}-x^2/r^2}\bigr)^2\bigr]^{1/2}}{(A+B)^{3/2}} .
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $U=\cos\psi N_0+\sin\psi B_0$, $W=T\times U$, $\kappa_\perp=\kappa_1\cos\psi+\kappa_2\sin\psi$, $\kappa_W=-\kappa_1\sin\psi+\kappa_2\cos\psi$ ($\kappa_\perp^2+\kappa_W^2=\kappa^2$), $x=r\kappa_\perp\in[-f,f]$. Differentiating \eqref{eq:deriv} with $U'=mW-\kappa_\perp vT$, $W'=-mU-\kappa_WvT$, $T'=v(\kappa_\perp U+\kappa_WW)$:
\begin{align*}
K_d'&=c_TT+c_WW,\qquad K_d''=aT+b_UU+b_WW,\\
c_T&=v(1-x),\qquad c_W=rm,\qquad b_U=v^2(1-x)\kappa_\perp-rm^2,\qquad b_W=v^2(1-x)\kappa_W,
\end{align*}
$a=a_0-2rvm\kappa_W$, $a_0=v'(1-x)-rv(\kappa_1'\cos\psi+\kappa_2'\sin\psi)$. Since $(T,U,W)$ is orthonormal and right-handed, $|K_d'\times K_d''|^2=b_U^2|K_d'|^2+(c_Wa-c_Tb_W)^2$. Put $A=v^2(1-x)^2$, $B=r^2m^2$, so $|K_d'|^2=A+B$ and $c_Wa-c_Tb_W=rma_0-\kappa_Wv(A+2B)$. By Minkowski,
$\kappa_{K_d}\le\sqrt{v^4(1-x)^2\kappa_\perp^2(A+B)+\kappa_W^2v^2(A+2B)^2}/(A+B)^{3/2}
+\bigl(rm^2(A+B)^{1/2}+rm|a_0|\bigr)/(A+B)^{3/2}$. In the first term
$A(A+B)\le(A+2B)^2$ and $\kappa_\perp^2+\kappa_W^2=\kappa^2$ give $\le v\kappa(A+2B)/(A+B)^{3/2}$, which is decreasing in $A\ge v^2(1-f)^2$; substituting $A=v^2(1-f)^2$ and $\zeta=v(1-f)/(rm)$ yields $\kappa\,g(\zeta)/(1-f)\le G(\zeta_{\min})/(\tau(1-f))$. The second term is at most $1/(r(1+\zeta_{\min}^2))+rm\,[M_v(1+f)+rv_{\max}M_\kappa]/(v_{\min}^2(1-f)^2+r^2m^2)^{3/2}$. (b) By the identity above, $\kappa_{K_d}^2=[b_U^2(A+B)+(rma_0-\kappa_Wv(A+2B))^2]/(A+B)^3$ with $x=r\kappa_\perp$, $|a_0|\le M_v(1-x)+rvM_\kappa$ and $|\kappa_W|\le(\tau^{-2}-x^2/r^2)^{1/2}$; for fixed $(x,v)$ this is non-decreasing in $|a_0|$ and $|\kappa_W|$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Same-strand pairs by a turning argument (added in v0.3).} For a pair on the same strand ($k=0$) Step~3 of Lemma~\ref{lem:Hc1} gives $|Q|\ge r[2|\sin(u/2)|\sqrt{1-\beta^2}-\delta^2/3]_+$, useless as $u\to0$, so short doubly critical pairs must be excluded otherwise. Version 0.2 did so with the curvature bound (a doubly critical arc of $K_d$ has length $\ge\pi/\bar\kappa_d$), which under $(\mathrm H_3)$ gave only $c_0=\HcCzeroHalfOne,\HcCzeroHalfTwo,\HcCzeroHalfThree$ at $f=1/2$, $d=1,2,3$. The following, obtained during the second round of review, measures instead the turning of the tangent of $K_d$ per unit base arclength and, for $d\ge2$, uses no derivative of the base; it supersedes the v0.2 proposition, which is omitted.

\begin{lemma}[turning exclusion]\label{lem:turn}\status{proved here}
Let $\Gamma$ be a $C^1$ arc from $X_1$ to $X_2$ with unit tangent $T_\Gamma$, $T_i$ its tangent at $X_i$, $a(\sigma)=\angle(T_\Gamma(\sigma),T_1)$ and $b(\sigma)=\angle(T_\Gamma(\sigma),T_2)$. If $a+b<\pi$ along $\Gamma$, then $(X_2-X_1)\cdot(T_1+T_2)>0$; in particular $(X_1,X_2)$ is not doubly critical. This holds whenever $\Gamma$ is $C^{1,1}$ and its total turning $\int_\Gamma|T_\Gamma'|$ is $<\pi$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
As $a,b\ge0$ and $a+b<\pi$, $\cos a+\cos b=2\cos\frac{a+b}2\cos\frac{a-b}2>0$, so $(X_2-X_1)\cdot(T_1+T_2)=\int_\Gamma T_\Gamma\cdot(T_1+T_2)\,d\sigma>0$, while a doubly critical pair has $(X_2-X_1)\cdot T_1=(X_2-X_1)\cdot T_2=0$. The last sentence follows from $a(\sigma)\le\int_0^\sigma|T_\Gamma'|$ and $b(\sigma)\le\int_\sigma^{\ell}|T_\Gamma'|$ ($T_\Gamma$ is Lipschitz, hence absolutely continuous).
\end{proof}

In the notation of the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:Hccurv}, $K_d'=v(1-x)\,T+rm\,W$ with $x=r\kappa_\perp\in[-f,f]$, so the angle $\chi\in(0,\pi/2)$ between $K_d'$ and $T$ has $\tan\chi=rm/(v(1-x))$ and $\chi_{\rm lo}:=\arctan\frac{rm}{v_{\max}(1+f)}\le\chi\le\chi_{\rm hi}:=\arctan\frac{rm}{v_{\min}(1-f)}=\arctan\frac1{\zeta_{\min}}$.

\begin{proposition}[pairs on the same strand, $p=2$, sharpened]\label{prop:samestrand}\status{proved here}
Let $p=2$, $q$ odd, $f=r/\tau\le\frac12$, $m>0$. Set $\delta_{R2}:=\bigl(\pi-2(\chi_{\rm hi}-\chi_{\rm lo})\bigr)/\bigl(1+\sin\chi_{\rm hi}/\Theta_{\min}\bigr)$; if $(\mathrm H_3)$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:Hccurv} holds, set $\delta_{R1}:=\pi/(\tau\bar\Omega)$ with
\[
\tau\bar\Omega:=1+\sin^2\chi_{\rm hi}+\frac{\sin\chi_{\rm hi}}{\Theta_{\min}}+\tau\,\frac{rm\,[M_v(1+f)/v_{\min}+rM_\kappa]}{v_{\min}^2(1-f)^2+r^2m^2},
\]
and otherwise $\delta_{R1}:=0$; let $\delta_{\rm turn}=\max(\delta_{R1},\delta_{R2})$. Then every doubly critical pair of $K_d$ with distinct base points and strand index $k=0$ relative to the shorter base arc has $\delta\ge\delta_{\rm turn}$ and
\[
|X_2-X_1|\ \ge\ \min\bigl(2(\tau-r),\ 2c_0'r\bigr),\qquad c_0':=\frac1{2r}\inf_{\delta_{\rm turn}\le\delta<\pi}\max\bigl(\Lambda(\delta),\,\ell_0(\delta)-2r\bigr).
\]
In particular $c_0'\ge\frac12$ whenever $\delta_{\rm turn}\ge\pi/3$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $\delta\ge\pi$ or the base chord is $\ge2\tau$, Step~1 of Lemma~\ref{lem:Hc1} and Proposition~\ref{prop:partial}(b) give $|X_2-X_1|\ge2(\tau-r)$. Otherwise let $\Gamma$ be the arc of $K_d$ over the shorter base arc, $t\in[t_1,t_2]$, so $u=m(t_2-t_1)\le\delta/\Theta_{\min}$; since $k=0$, $\Gamma$ joins $X_1$ to $X_2$.

\emph{$\delta\ge\delta_{R2}$, without $(\mathrm H_3)$.} Put $\chi_i=\chi(t_i)$ and $Y_i(t)=\cos\chi_i\,T(t)+\sin\chi_i\,W(t)$. From $U'=mW-v\kappa_\perp T$, $W'=-mU-v\kappa_WT$, $T'=v(\kappa_\perp U+\kappa_WW)$ we get $Y_i'=(v\kappa_\perp\cos\chi_i-m\sin\chi_i)U+v\kappa_W\cos\chi_i\,W-v\kappa_W\sin\chi_i\,T$ and $|Y_i'|^2=(v\kappa_\perp\cos\chi_i-m\sin\chi_i)^2+v^2\kappa_W^2\le(v\kappa+m\sin\chi_i)^2$, using $\kappa_\perp^2\cos^2\chi_i+\kappa_W^2\le\kappa^2$ and $-\kappa_\perp\cos\chi_i\le\kappa$. The unit tangent $T_d(t)=\cos\chi(t)\,T(t)+\sin\chi(t)\,W(t)$ and $Y_i(t)$ lie in $\operatorname{span}(T(t),W(t))$ at angles $\chi(t)$, $\chi_i$ from $T(t)$, and $Y_i(t_i)=T_d(t_i)$, so $a(t)\le|\chi(t)-\chi_1|+\int_{t_1}^{t}(v\kappa+m\sin\chi_1)$ and $b(t)\le|\chi(t)-\chi_2|+\int_{t}^{t_2}(v\kappa+m\sin\chi_2)$. With $\int v\kappa\,dt\le\sigma/\tau=\delta$, $a+b\le2(\chi_{\rm hi}-\chi_{\rm lo})+\delta+u\sin\chi_{\rm hi}\le2(\chi_{\rm hi}-\chi_{\rm lo})+\delta(1+\sin\chi_{\rm hi}/\Theta_{\min})$, which is $<\pi$ if $\delta<\delta_{R2}$; Lemma~\ref{lem:turn} excludes the pair.

\emph{$\delta\ge\delta_{R1}$, under $(\mathrm H_3)$.} The turning rate of $T_d$ per unit $t$ is $\varpi=|K_d'\times K_d''|/|K_d'|^2$. The Minkowski split in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:Hccurv}, multiplied by $|K_d'|=\sqrt{A+B}$, gives $\varpi\le v\kappa(A+2B)/(A+B)+rm^2/\sqrt{A+B}+rm|a_0|/(A+B)$, where $B/(A+B)=\sin^2\chi$ and $rm/\sqrt{A+B}=\sin\chi$; with $\kappa\le1/\tau$, $\chi\le\chi_{\rm hi}$, $v\ge v_{\min}$ and $|a_0|\le M_v(1+f)+rvM_\kappa$ this is $\varpi/v\le\bar\Omega$ per unit base arclength, so the total turning of $\Gamma$ is $\le\tau\bar\Omega\,\delta$, which is $<\pi$ if $\delta<\delta_{R1}$.

\emph{Distance.} For $\delta<\pi$, Step~3 of Lemma~\ref{lem:Hc1}, which does not use the strand index, gives $|X_2-X_1|\ge\Lambda(\delta)$ and $|X_2-X_1|\ge\ell_0(\delta)-2r$; hence $c_0'$. Finally $\Lambda\ge r$ on $[\pi/3,\pi)$ when $f\le\frac12$: on $[\pi/3,\pi/2]$, $\Lambda=2(\tau-r)\sin(\delta/2)\ge\tau-r\ge r$; on $[\pi/2,\pi)$ this is Step~4 of Lemma~\ref{lem:Hc1}, and $\Lambda/r$ decreases in $f$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[same strand in the default family]\label{cor:samefamily}\status{proved here}
Let $(p_j,q_j)=(2,3)$ and $f_j=r_j/\thick(K_{j-1})\le\frac12$ for all $j\le d$. Then every doubly critical pair of $K_d$ with distinct base points on the same strand is at distance $\ge\min(2(\tau-r),2c_0'r)$ with $c_0'\ge\frac12$, at every depth $d\ge1$, without $(\mathrm H_3)$. At $d=1$ and $f=\frac12$, $\delta_{\rm turn}\ge\delta_{R1}=\pi/(1+\frac9{13}+\frac9{2\sqrt{13}})>\SsDeltaROneHalf$ and $c_0'\ge\sin(\delta_{R1}/2)>\SsSinHalf$ (and $c_0'>\SsSinHalf$ for every $f\le\frac12$); for $d\ge2$, $\delta_{\rm turn}\ge\delta_{R2}>\SsDeltaRTwoAp$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
\emph{$d=1$.} The base is the unit circle: $v\equiv1$, $\kappa\equiv1=1/\tau$, $\alpha=0$, $m=\frac32$, $\Theta_{\min}=\frac23$, and $(\mathrm H_3)$ holds with $M_v=M_\kappa=0$. With $\zeta=\zeta_{\min}=2(1-f)/(3f)$, $\tau\bar\Omega=1+\frac1{1+\zeta^2}+\frac{3}{2\sqrt{1+\zeta^2}}$, which is $<\SsTauOmegaHalf<3$ at $f=\frac12$ ($\zeta=\frac23$) and decreases as $f$ decreases; so $\delta_{R1}(f)\ge\delta_{R1}(\frac12)>\pi/3$. We argue at $f=\frac12$, with $c_\ast:=2\sin(\delta_{R1}(\frac12)/2)$ fixed. On $[\delta_{R1}(\frac12),\pi/2]$, $\Lambda/r=2(1/f-1)\sin(\delta/2)\ge c_\ast$. On $[\pi/2,\pi)$ the argument of Step~4 of Lemma~\ref{lem:Hc1} with $1$ replaced by $c_\ast$ goes through: $\phi_{c_\ast}(\varsigma)=4\varsigma^2-c_\ast\varsigma-1+\pi/2-2\arcsin\varsigma$ has the same second derivative, $\phi_{c_\ast}'(1/\sqrt2)=2\sqrt2-c_\ast>\SsPhiPrime$, $\phi_{c_\ast}(1/\sqrt2)=1-c_\ast/\sqrt2>\SsPhiA$ and $\phi_{c_\ast}(1)=3-c_\ast-\pi/2>\SsPhiB$; so $c_0'(\frac12)\ge c_\ast/2>\SsSinHalf$. For $f<\frac12$ the interval $[\delta_{\rm turn}(f),\pi)$ is contained in $[\delta_{R1}(\frac12),\pi)$ and $\Lambda/r$ increases as $f$ decreases, so $c_0'(f)\ge c_\ast/2$ as well.

\emph{$d\ge2$.} By \eqref{eq:deriv}, $|K_1'|^2=(1-x)^2+r_1^2m_1^2$ with $r_1=f_1$, $m_1=\frac32$, so after the reparametrisation $v_{\min}(K_1)\ge2\sqrt{(1-f_1)^2+\frac94f_1^2}$, while $\thick(K_1)\le f_1$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:upper}); hence $v_{\min}(K_1)/\thick(K_1)\ge2\sqrt{(1-f_1)^2/f_1^2+\frac94}\ge\sqrt{13}$. For $j\ge2$, $v_{\min}(K_j)\ge2(1-f_j)v_{\min}(K_{j-1})$ and $\thick(K_j)\le f_j\thick(K_{j-1})$, so $v_{\min}/\thick$ at least doubles per depth. With $m_d\le\frac32+\frac12=2$, $\Theta_{\min}\ge\sqrt{13}\,2^{d-3}\ge\sqrt{13}/2>\SsThetaAp$ and $\zeta_{\min}=(1-f_d)\Theta_{\min}/f_d\ge\Theta_{\min}$. Dropping $\chi_{\rm lo}\ge0$, $\delta_{R2}\ge(\pi-2\arctan(1/\zeta_{\min}))/(1+1/(\Theta_{\min}\sqrt{1+\zeta_{\min}^2}))$, which increases in $\Theta_{\min}$ and $\zeta_{\min}$ and at $\Theta_{\min}=\zeta_{\min}=\sqrt{13}/2$ exceeds $\SsDeltaRTwoAp>\pi/3$. (The bound $\Theta_{\min}\ge2^{d-2}$ of Step~5 of Lemma~\ref{lem:Hc1} would not suffice at $d=2$: at $f=\frac12$, $\delta_{R2}\ge\pi/3$ needs $\Theta_{\min}\ge\SsThetaStar$.) Proposition~\ref{prop:samestrand} concludes.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thickness lower bound for $p=2$]\label{thm:Hc}\status{proved here; (b) under $(\mathrm H_3)$; the values in Table~\ref{tab:consts} are grid evaluations, not certified}
Let $p=2$, $q$ odd, $f=r/\tau\le1/2$, $m>0$. (a) $\frac12\dcsd(K_d)\ge\min\bigl(\tau-r,\ \min(c_1,c_0')\,r\bigr)$, with $c_1=c_1(f,\Theta_{\min})$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:Hc1} and $c_0'$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:samestrand}. (b) Under $(\mathrm H_3)$, $\minRad(K_d)\ge1/\hat\kappa_d$, hence $\thick(K_d)\ge\min(\tau-r,\ cr)$ with $c=\min(c_1,c_0',1/(r\hat\kappa_d))$. (c) For the default family $(2,3)$ with $f_j\le\frac12$ at every level, $\frac12\dcsd(K_d)\ge\min(\tau-r,\,r/2)=r/2$ and $\thick(K_d)\ge\min(\minRad(K_d),\,r/2)$ at every depth, without $(\mathrm H_3)$; at $d=1$, $\thick(K_1)\ge r_1/2$; for $d\ge2$, $(\mathrm H_c)$ with $c=\frac12$ holds at level $d$ if and only if $\minRad(K_d)\ge r_d/2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:lsdr}, $\thick=\min(\minRad,\frac12\dcsd)$. Doubly critical pairs with the same base point are at distance $2r$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:partial}(a)); if the shorter base arc has $\delta\ge\pi$, the base chord is $\ge2\tau$ (Step~1 of Lemma~\ref{lem:Hc1}) and the distance is $\ge2(\tau-r)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:partial}(b)); pairs with $\delta<\pi$ are covered by Lemma~\ref{lem:Hc1} ($k=1$) and Proposition~\ref{prop:samestrand} ($k=0$). Since $c_1\le1$ (its limit as $\delta\to0$ is $1$), this gives (a); (b) is Proposition~\ref{prop:Hccurv}(b). For (c), $c_1\ge\frac12$ is Lemma~\ref{lem:Hc1}, $c_0'\ge\frac12$ is Corollary~\ref{cor:samefamily}, and $\tau-r\ge r$. At $d=1$ ($M_v=M_\kappa=0$, $v\equiv1$), Proposition~\ref{prop:Hccurv} gives $r\bar\kappa_1=fG(\zeta_{\min})/(1-f)+1/(1+\zeta_{\min}^2)$ with $\zeta_{\min}=2(1-f)/(3f)$ and $G\le\max g=g(\sqrt2)=4\sqrt2/(3\sqrt3)$ (the derivative of $y(y+2)^2/(y+1)^3$, $y=\zeta^2$, is proportional to $(y+2)(2-y)$); this increases with $f$, and at $f=\frac12$ equals $g(\sqrt2)+\frac9{13}$, so $1/(r\bar\kappa_1)>\KbarOneHalf>\frac12$. The equivalence for $d\ge2$ follows from $\thick=\min(\minRad,\frac12\dcsd)$, $\frac12\dcsd\ge r/2$ and $\min(\tau-r,\frac r2)=\frac r2$.
\end{proof}

Without the refinements of this version one has at $f=1/2$, $d=1,2,3$ only $c_0=\HcCzeroHalfOne,\HcCzeroHalfTwo,\HcCzeroHalfThree$ and $1/(r\bar\kappa_d)=\HcCkappaHalfOne,\HcCkappaHalfTwo,\HcCkappaHalfThree$. In Table~\ref{tab:consts}, $c\ge\SsCHalfOne$ in every measured level, and the minimum is the different-strand constant $c_1$ except at $(f=\frac12,d=1)$, where it is $c_0'$.

\begin{remark}[no constant works for an arbitrary base]\label{rem:Hcfalse}\status{proved here}
No $c>0$ depending only on $(p,q,f)$ makes $\thick(K_d)\ge\min(\tau-r,cr\sin(\pi/p))$ true for every smooth base $K$ with $\thick(K)=\tau$. (i) If the curvature of $K$ rises from $0$ to $1/\tau$ over an arc of length $\varepsilon$ (a smoothed junction of a segment and a circle of radius $\tau$), by \eqref{eq:deriv} the tangent of the inner strand turns from $vT+rmW$ to $v(1-f)T+rmW$ over that arc, so $\kappa_{K_d}\gtrsim rv\kappa'/|K_d'|^2\to\infty$: the $M_v$, $M_\kappa$ term of \eqref{eq:Hckappa} is unavoidable. (ii) If $K$ is traversed slowly ($v\to0$) on an arc of length $\gg r$, the two strands there are helices of pitch $h=v/m\to0$; in the straight-tube model $|K_d(t)-K_d(t+2\pi+u)|^2=4r^2\cos^2(qu/4)+v^2u^2$, whose minimum over $u$ is $\le(2\pi v/q)^2$ and is attained at a doubly critical pair by screw symmetry, so $\thick(K_d)\le\pi v/q\to0$ (in Lemma~\ref{lem:Hc1}, $c_1\to0$ as $\Theta_{\min}\to0$). In the family $\Theta_{\min}$ is bounded below a priori (Lemma~\ref{lem:Hc1}, Corollary~\ref{cor:samefamily}); no a priori bound on $M_v$, $M_\kappa$ uniform in $d$ is known (but see Corollary~\ref{cor:tolerance}).
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Towards $(\mathrm H_3)$ uniformly in $d$ (added in v0.4).} For a regular closed curve $K$ with parameter $t$ of period $2\pi$, speed $v$, curvature vector $\boldsymbol\kappa=dT/d\sigma=\kappa_1N_0+\kappa_2B_0$ and $\kappa_{\rm par}':=\kappa_1'N_0+\kappa_2'B_0$ (the normal part of $d\boldsymbol\kappa/dt$), set $\nu(K):=\sup_t|v'|/v^2$ and $\mu(K):=\sup_t|\kappa_{\rm par}'|/v$. $\nu$ is invariant under linear reparametrisations ($\tilde v(s)=p\,v(ps)$ gives $\tilde v'/\tilde v^2=v'/v^2$), $\mu=\sup|\Pi_\perp d\boldsymbol\kappa/d\sigma|$ is geometric, and $M_v\le\nu v_{\max}^2$, $M_\kappa\le\mu v_{\max}$. In the notation of the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:Hccurv} put $\varepsilon:=r\kappa_{\max}(K)\le f$, $\lambda:=rm/v_{\min}$ and $\zeta':=v_{\min}(1-\varepsilon)/(rm)\ge\zeta_{\min}$. The four statements below were derived in \texttt{theory/} after the second review, checked by the author (symbolic frame calculus and a smooth computation, \texttt{experiments/verify\_H3.py}) and by an independent internal referee (round 4).

\begin{lemma}[speed of $K_d$]\label{lem:speedrec}\status{proved here; checked by an independent internal referee (round 4)}
With $w=|K_d'|$, $c_T=v(1-x)$ and $a_0=v'(1-x)-rv\,\kappa_{\rm par}'\cdot U$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wprime}
w'=\frac{c_T\,(a_0-rvm\kappa_W)}{w},
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
\nu(K_d)\ \le\ \frac{\nu(K)}{1-\varepsilon}+\frac{r\mu(K)+\lambda\,\kappa_{\max}(K)}{(1-\varepsilon)^2}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$w^2=c_T^2+r^2m^2$ with $rm$ constant, so $w'=c_Tc_T'/w$, and $c_T'=v'(1-x)-vx'$ with $x'=r(\kappa_{\rm par}'\cdot U+m\kappa_W)$ (as $\boldsymbol\kappa\cdot U'=m\kappa_W$). Then $|w'|/w^2\le|a_0-rvm\kappa_W|/c_T^2$ (since $w\ge c_T$), and $|v'|\le\nu v^2$, $|\kappa_{\rm par}'|\le\mu v$, $|\kappa_W|\le\kappa_{\max}$, $1-x\ge1-\varepsilon$, $v\ge v_{\min}$ give the bound; $\nu(K_d)$ may be computed in any linear reparametrisation.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[curvature of $K_d$ from the data of the base]\label{prop:curvrec}\status{proved here; checked by an independent internal referee (round 4)}
Under the hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{prop:Hccurv}, with $G$ as there,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kaprec}
\kappa_{\max}(K_d)\ \le\ \frac{\kappa_{\max}(K)\,G(\zeta')}{1-\varepsilon}+\frac1{r(1+\zeta'^2)}+\frac{\lambda\,\bigl(\nu(K)(1+\varepsilon)+r\mu(K)\bigr)}{(1-\varepsilon)^3}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The Minkowski split of the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:Hccurv}(a) with $|x|\le\varepsilon$ and $\kappa\le\kappa_{\max}(K)$: the first term is $\le v\kappa(A+2B)/(A+B)^{3/2}$, decreasing in $A\ge v^2(1-\varepsilon)^2$, hence $\le\kappa_{\max}G(\zeta')/(1-\varepsilon)$; $rm^2/(A+B)\le1/(r(1+\zeta'^2))$; and $rm|a_0|/(A+B)^{3/2}\le rm\,v^2(\nu(1+\varepsilon)+r\mu)/(v^3(1-\varepsilon)^3)\le\lambda(\nu(1+\varepsilon)+r\mu)/(1-\varepsilon)^3$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[tolerated growth of the $(\mathrm H_3)$ data, default family]\label{cor:tolerance}\status{proved here; checked by an independent internal referee (round 4)}
Let $(p_j,q_j)=(2,3)$ and $r_j\le f\,\thick(K_{j-1})$, $f\le\frac12$, for all $j\le d$, and let $d\ge3$. If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tol}
\frac{96}{\sqrt{13}}\,4^{-d}\,\nu(K_{d-1})+\frac{64}{\sqrt{13}}\,8^{-d}\,\mu(K_{d-1})\ \le\ 2-g(\sqrt{13})-\tfrac1{14}\quad(=\HthreeTolRHS\ldots),
\end{equation}
then $\minRad(K_d)\ge r_d/2$, hence $\thick(K_d)\ge r_d/2$: \eqref{eq:rho} below holds at depth $d$ with $c=\frac12$. (Each term may take the whole right-hand side only if the other vanishes: $\nu(K_{d-1})\le\HthreeNuTol\cdot4^d$ with $\mu=0$, or $\mu(K_{d-1})\le\HthreeMuTol\cdot8^d$ with $\nu=0$; in general the joint condition \eqref{eq:tol} applies.)
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By the proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:samefamily}, $\zeta'\ge\zeta_{\min}\ge\Theta_{\min}\ge\sqrt{13}\,2^{d-3}\ge\sqrt{13}$, so $G(\zeta')\le g(\sqrt{13})$ ($g$ decreases on $[\sqrt2,\infty)$) and $1/(1+\zeta'^2)\le1/14$; $\varepsilon\le\frac12$ gives $\varepsilon/(1-\varepsilon)\le1$, $(1+\varepsilon)/(1-\varepsilon)^3\le12$, $(1-\varepsilon)^{-3}\le8$; Proposition~\ref{prop:upper} gives $r_d\le2^{-d}$; and $\lambda=(1-f_d)/\zeta_{\min}\le2^{3-d}/\sqrt{13}$. Multiplying \eqref{eq:kaprec} by $r_d$, $r_d\kappa_{\max}(K_d)\le g(\sqrt{13})+\frac1{14}+12r_d\lambda\nu+8r_d^2\lambda\mu\le2$ under \eqref{eq:tol}; Theorem~\ref{thm:Hc}(c) concludes.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[the recursion for $(\mathrm H_3)$ does not close]\label{prop:noclose}\status{proved here; checked by an independent internal referee (round 4)}
Fourth derivatives of $K$ enter $K_d'''=K'''+rU'''$ only through the term $-rv\,(\kappa_{\rm par}''\cdot U)\,T$ of $rU'''$: with $n_\chi:=\sin\chi\,T-\cos\chi\,W$ the unit normal of $K_d$ in $\operatorname{span}(T,W)$ and $\sin\chi=rm/w>0$, the normal part of $K_d'''$ is $-rv\sin\chi\,(\kappa_{\rm par}''\cdot U)\,n_\chi+R$, with $|R|$ bounded in terms of $v_{\max},\kappa_{\max},\nu,\mu,\sup|v''|,r,m$. Consequently no locally bounded $F$ satisfies $\mu(K_d)\le F(v_{\min},v_{\max},\kappa_{\max},\nu,\mu,\sup|v''|,r,m)$ for all smooth bases $K$: the speed and curvature of $K_d$ are controlled by the $(\mathrm H_3)$ data of $K$, but $\mu(K_d)$ is not.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$U'''=mW''-(v\kappa_\perp)''T-2(v\kappa_\perp)'T'-v\kappa_\perp T''$, where $W''=-mU'-(v\kappa_W)'T-v\kappa_WT'$ and $T''=v'\boldsymbol\kappa+v\,d\boldsymbol\kappa/dt$ involve at most third derivatives of $K$, and $\kappa_\perp''=\kappa_{\rm par}''\cdot U+2m\,\kappa_{\rm par}'\cdot W-m^2\kappa_\perp$; the normal part of $T$ is $\sin\chi\,n_\chi$ (a symbolic computation in the frame $(T,U,W)$ confirms the coefficient; $v''$ enters $R$ with coefficient $(1-x)\sin\chi$). For the last claim take a smooth closed $K$ with $r<\thick(K)$ and $K_\eta=K+\eta^{7/2}\Phi((t-t_0)/\eta)\,U(t_0)$ with $\Phi$ smooth, compactly supported, $\Phi''''(0)\ne0$. For small $\eta$ the support lies in one period, $K_\eta$ is regular and $r<\thick(K_\eta)$, so $K_{d,\eta}$ is defined and embedded (thickness is lower semicontinuous under $C^2$ convergence: $\minRad$ is continuous, and doubly critical pairs at distance $\le2r$ converge, after extraction, to a doubly critical pair of $K$, collapsing pairs being excluded by Lemma~\ref{lem:turn}). $K_\eta\to K$ in $C^3$, so the arguments of $F$ converge, while $|\kappa_{\rm par}''\cdot U|(t_0)\gtrsim\eta^{-1/2}$; since the normal part of $K_d'''$ is $3ww'\boldsymbol\kappa_d+w^3\Pi_\perp d\boldsymbol\kappa_d/d\sigma$ with $w$, $w'$, $\kappa_{K_d}$ bounded by Lemma~\ref{lem:speedrec} and Proposition~\ref{prop:curvrec}, $\mu(K_{d,\eta})\to\infty$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[what this gives and what it does not]\label{rem:H3status}\status{grid evaluation with polygon inputs; uniformity open}
Lemma~\ref{lem:speedrec} and Proposition~\ref{prop:curvrec} propagate $\nu$ and $\kappa_{\max}$ given $\mu$ at the previous depth, and Corollary~\ref{cor:tolerance} reduces $(\mathrm H_c)$ with $c=\frac12$ at depth $d\ge3$ to a growth condition. This does \emph{not} prove $(\mathrm H_c)$ for $d\ge3$: by Proposition~\ref{prop:noclose} an a priori bound on $\mu(K_{d-1})$ cannot be obtained from the $(\mathrm H_3)$ data of $K_{d-2}$ alone; a recursive argument must carry higher derivatives (e.g.\ all of them, via analyticity) or use the explicit structure of the family (one derivative enters per depth, with coefficient $r_d\sin\chi_d\le\HthreeLossConst\cdot4^{-d}$ a priori; on the polygons $r_d\sin\chi_d=\HthreeLossTwo,\HthreeLossThree,\HthreeLossFour$ at $d=2,3,4$, roughly $8^{-d}$). Proposition~\ref{prop:noclose} says nothing about the family itself, where $\mu$ grows slowly (values below). At $d=2$ the a priori constants do not suffice: the analogue of \eqref{eq:tol} would need $\frac6{\sqrt{13}}\nu(K_1)+\frac1{\sqrt{13}}\mu(K_1)\le2-g(\frac{\sqrt{13}}2)-\frac4{17}<\HthreeDtwoRHS$, while the polygon values give $\HthreeDtwoLHS$; $d=2$ is settled instead by Proposition~\ref{prop:d2cert}. On the polygons ($f=\frac12$, $N_0=256$; $d=4$ at $N_0=128$, slightly below the smooth values $\HthreeNuFourSmooth$, $\HthreeMuFourSmooth$ of \texttt{experiments/smooth\_H3.py}): $\nu=\HthreeNuOne,\HthreeNuTwo,\HthreeNuThree,\HthreeNuFour$ and $\mu=\HthreeMuOne,\HthreeMuTwo,\HthreeMuThree,\HthreeMuFour$ at $d=1,\dots,4$; the left-hand side of \eqref{eq:tol} is $\le\HthreeTapThree$ ($d=3$) and $\le\HthreeTapFour$ ($d=4$; $\le\HthreeTapFourSmooth$ with the smooth data); $r_d$ times the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:kaprec} with measured inputs is $\le\HthreeSharpTwo,\HthreeSharpThree,\HthreeSharpFour$ at $d=2,3,4$, against the requirement $\le2$; and \eqref{eq:wprime}, \eqref{eq:kaprec} hold in all $\HthreeNumLevels$ checked levels ($f\in\{0.25,0.35,0.5\}$) with bound/measured $\ge\HthreeMinBoundRatio$ (\texttt{experiments/check\_H3.py}).
\end{remark}

\paragraph{The case $d=2$ (added in v0.5).} At $d=2$ the base $K_1$ is an explicit trigonometric polynomial, so instead of the a priori constants one can insert into \eqref{eq:kaprec} rigorous enclosures of the four numbers of $K_1$ that it uses.

\begin{proposition}[the case $d=2$]\label{prop:d2cert}\status{computer-assisted proof (interval arithmetic); not yet checked by an independent referee}
Let $(p_1,q_1)=(p_2,q_2)=(2,3)$, $0<r_1\le\frac12$ and $0<r_2\le r_1/2$, with arbitrary phases $\varphi_1,\varphi_2$ (equivalently, arbitrary initial normals of the frames of $K_0$ and $K_1$). Then
\[
r_2\,\kappa_{\max}(K_2)\ \le\ \DtwoCertBound,\qquad\text{hence}\qquad \minRad(K_2)\ \ge\ \DtwoCertMinRad\,r_2\ >\ r_2/2 .
\]
Consequently, for every $f\le\frac12$, every $r_1\le f$ and every $r_2\le f\,\thick(K_1)$, $\thick(K_2)\ge r_2/2$: Hypothesis~\ref{hyp} with $c=\frac12$ holds at $d=2$ for every choice of phases. Along the family $r_1=f$, $r_2\le f^2$ one has moreover $\minRad(K_2)>r_2$ for every $f\le\DtwoCertFc$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Reduction.} By Proposition~\ref{prop:curvrec} with $K=K_1$, $r=r_2$, $r_2\kappa_{\max}(K_2)\le\Phi$, where $\Phi$ is $r_2$ times the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:kaprec}, a function of $\kappa_{\max}(K_1)$, $v_{\min}(K_1)$, $\nu(K_1)$, $\mu(K_1)$, $r_2$ and $m_2$; that proof uses these numbers only through $|\boldsymbol\kappa|\le\kappa_{\max}$, $|v'|\le\nu v^2$, $|\kappa_{\rm par}'|\le\mu v$, $v\ge v_{\min}$, so upper bounds of $\kappa_{\max},\nu,\mu$ and a lower bound of $v_{\min}$ may be inserted. $\Phi$ is non-decreasing in $\kappa_{\max}$, $\nu$, $\mu$, $r_2$ and $m_2$ and non-increasing in $v_{\min}$: $\varepsilon=r_2\kappa_{\max}$ and $\lambda=r_2m_2/v_{\min}$ increase, $\zeta'=v_{\min}(1-\varepsilon)/(r_2m_2)$ decreases, and $G$ is non-increasing. Since $\alpha_2\in(-\pi,\pi]$, $m_2=\frac32-\alpha_2/2\pi<2$, so $m_2$ may be replaced by $2$: the Bishop frame of $K_1$ does not enter. By Proposition~\ref{prop:upper}, $\thick(K_1)\le r_1$, so $r_2\le f\thick(K_1)$ with $f\le\frac12$ implies $r_2\le r_1/2$. A change of $\varphi_1$ moves $K_1$ by a rigid motion and a shift of the parameter ($K_1^{\varphi}(s)=R_z(-2\varphi/3)K_1^{0}(s+\varphi/3)$), which leave the four numbers unchanged; $\varphi_2$ enters \eqref{eq:kaprec} only through $|U|=1$.

\emph{The base.} With $t=2s$, $K_1(s)=\bigl(e^{2is}(1+r_1\sin3s),\ r_1\cos3s\bigr)$ (first two coordinates as a complex number), i.e.\ $x+iy=e^{2is}+\frac{r_1}{2i}(e^{5is}-e^{-is})$, whose derivatives $D_j=d^jK_1/ds^j$ are explicit. With $v=|D_1|$: $\kappa=|D_1\times D_2|/v^3$, $|v'|/v^2=|D_1\cdot D_2|/v^3$ and, from $\Pi_\perp K_1'''=3vv'\boldsymbol\kappa+v^2\Pi_\perp\boldsymbol\kappa'$ and $\boldsymbol\kappa=\Pi_\perp D_2/v^2$, $|\kappa_{\rm par}'|/v=\bigl|\Pi_\perp D_3-3(D_1\cdot D_2)\,\Pi_\perp D_2/v^2\bigr|/v^3$, where $\Pi_\perp$ is the projection orthogonal to $D_1$. Moreover $K_1(s+2\pi/3)=R_z(4\pi/3)K_1(s)$ exactly, so suprema over $[0,2\pi]$ equal suprema over $[0,2\pi/3]$.

\emph{Enclosure.} Cover $[0,\frac12]\times[0,2\pi/3]$ by $\DtwoCertNrB\times\DtwoCertNsB$ boxes in $(r_1,s)$ ($r_1$-endpoints exact binary fractions, $s$-endpoints enclosed in \texttt{mpmath.iv}); on each box enclose $\cos ks,\sin ks$ ($k=1,2,3,5$) with \texttt{mpmath.iv} (80 bits, rounded outward to binary64), and evaluate the expressions above in binary64 interval arithmetic with every endpoint rounded outward by one ulp after each IEEE-754 operation ($+,-,\times,\div,\sqrt{\ }$), which encloses the exact value. On each $r_1$-box $[a,b]$ this gives upper bounds of $\kappa_{\max}$, $\nu$, $\mu$ and a lower bound of $v_{\min}$ over the box; $\Phi$ evaluated in the same arithmetic at these values with $r_2=b/2$ and $m_2=2$ is at most $\DtwoCertBound$ on every box (the maximum is on the last box, $r_1\in[0.499,0.5]$). Theorem~\ref{thm:Hc}(c) gives $\thick(K_2)\ge r_2/2$. For the last claim the same box data with $r_2=b^2$ give $\Phi<1$ on every box with $b\le\DtwoCertFc$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[what is certified, and how]\label{rem:d2method}
The certificate covers exactly $r_2\kappa_{\max}(K_2)\le\DtwoCertBound$ for $r_1\le\frac12$, $r_2\le r_1/2$ and all phases; with Theorem~\ref{thm:Hc}(c) (proved by hand) this settles $d=2$. A second computation over the full period ($\DtwoCertNs$ boxes, no symmetry) gives at $r_1=\frac12$: $v_{\min}(K_1)\ge\DtwoCertVmin$, $\kappa_{\max}(K_1)\le\DtwoCertKmax$, $\nu(K_1)\le\DtwoCertNu$, $\mu(K_1)\le\DtwoCertMu$ and $\Phi\le\DtwoCertBoundHalf$ ($r_2\le\frac14$); at $f=0.35$, $0.25$ ($r_2\le f^2$), $\Phi\le\DtwoCertBoundTF$, $\le\DtwoCertBoundQ$. The trust base is IEEE-754 binary64 arithmetic in NumPy, \texttt{mpmath.iv}, and about one hundred lines of code implementing the formulas above (\texttt{experiments/certify\_d2.py}, a frozen copy of the script of \texttt{theory/}; $\DtwoCertCPU$\,s); it is not a formal verification. An independent check of the implementation by the author (\texttt{experiments/check\_certify\_d2.py}: other formulas for $\nu$, $\mu$, point values on the full period inside every box enclosure, $\DtwoCheckPts$ point-in-box tests, a unit test of the interval class against exact rationals, $\Phi$ recomputed in 50-digit arithmetic) found no violation. The bound is not sharp: \eqref{eq:kaprec} takes the four suprema separately and uses $m_2\le2$; the smooth (uncertified) value of $r_2\kappa_{\max}(K_2)$ at $f=\frac12$, $r_2=\frac14$ and default phases is $\DtwoSmoothRK$ (\texttt{verify\_H3.py}) against $\DtwoCertBoundHalf$. This is why $\minRad(K_2)>r_2$ is obtained only for $f\le\DtwoCertFc$. Nothing at $d\ge3$, and no polygonal quantity, is certified.
\end{remark}

What remains unproved is therefore a statement about the family, not about an arbitrary curve:

\begin{hypothesis}[$\mathrm H_c$, for the family]\label{hyp}\status{for $(2,3)$ and $c=1/2$: proved at $d=1$; reduced to a curvature bound for $d\ge2$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:Hc}) and, for $d\ge3$, to a growth condition (Corollary~\ref{cor:tolerance}); verified numerically}
Let $(K_d)$ be the family of Definition~\ref{def:family} built from the circle with a fixed pattern $(p,q)$, and let $f\le1/2$. There is $c=c(p,q,f)\in(0,1]$ such that, for every sequence $(r_d)$ with $r_d\le f\,\thick(K_{d-1})$ and every $d\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rho}
\thick(K_d)\ \ge\ \min\bigl(\thick(K_{d-1})-r_d,\ c\,r_d\sin(\pi/p)\bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{hypothesis}

\noindent\emph{Status for $(2,3)$, $f\le1/2$, $c=1/2$.} By Theorem~\ref{thm:Hc}(c), \eqref{eq:rho} holds at $d=1$ and, for $d\ge2$, is equivalent to $\minRad(K_d)\ge r_d/2$. Under $(\mathrm H_3)$ this follows from $1/(r\hat\kappa_d)\ge1/2$, which the grid evaluation with polygon-measured inputs gives at $d=2,3$ ($\SsCkappaHalfTwo$, $\SsCkappaHalfThree$ at $f=1/2$; Table~\ref{tab:consts}); for $d\ge3$ it also follows from \eqref{eq:tol}, which the polygons satisfy at $d=3,4$ (Remark~\ref{rem:H3status}). This is evidence, not a proof: $\nu$, $\mu$ (and, for $\hat\kappa_d$, $\thick(K_{d-1})$) are only measured. Open: $(\mathrm H_3)$ uniformly in $d$ (here, a priori control of $\mu(K_{d-1})$), the case $d=2$, and certification. Numerically \eqref{eq:rho} with $c=1/2$ holds in all $\HypLevels$ default levels with margin $\tau_d/\rho_d\ge\HypMinTauOverRho$, and along the chain of Corollary~\ref{cor:rec} with margin $\ge\CorTauRhoMin$.

\paragraph{On the constant.} The natural constant is $c=1$: $r\sin(\pi/p)$ is half the strand chord, and by Propositions~\ref{prop:upper} and~\ref{prop:partial}(a) it cannot be improved where $\tau_d=r_d$. With $c=1$ the inequality fails on the polygons in $\HypFailOne$ of the $\HypLevelsAll$ measured levels with $f\le1/2$ (\HypFailOneList; the deficit at $(3,2)$, $d=2$ is stable under doubling of $N$); it holds with equality, to $\EqTolPct\%$, in every level with $\tau_d=r_d$ and in \HypEqOneListOther, where the critical pair is a pair of adjacent strands of one disc. The value $c=1/2$ was chosen after $c=1$ had failed (Section~\ref{sec:exp}); Theorem~\ref{thm:Hc} reaches it, while $\tau_1/r_1=\RatioHalfOne$ at $f=1/2$ shows that no argument reaches $c=1$ there.

\begin{corollary}[recursion for the ropelength bound]\label{cor:rec}\status{proved here, conditional on $\mathrm H_c$ for the family (for $(2,3)$, $c=1/2$: on $\minRad(K_d)\ge r_d/2$, $d\ge2$)}
Fix $(p,q)$, $f\le1/2$ and assume $(\mathrm H_c)$ with constant $c$ (for all $r_d\le f\,\thick(K_{d-1})$). Define $\rho_0=1$, $r_d:=f\rho_{d-1}$ and $\rho_d:=\gamma\rho_{d-1}$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:consts}
\gamma=\min\bigl(1-f,\ c f\sin(\pi/p)\bigr),\qquad \mathcal A=p(1+f)+f\bigl(q+\tfrac p2\bigr).
\end{equation}
Then $\thick(K_d)\ge\rho_d=\gamma^d$ and $L(K_d)\le2\pi\mathcal A^d$ for all $d$, hence $\Rop(K_d)\le2\pi\Lambda_{\Rop}^d$ with $\Lambda_{\Rop}=\mathcal A/\gamma$. For $(2,3)$ and $f=1/2$: $\mathcal A=\AconstHalf$, and $\Lambda_{\Rop}=\LambdaHalf$ with $c=1/2$ ($\gamma=\GammaHalf$), $\Lambda_{\Rop}=\LambdaOneHalf$ with $c=1$ ($\gamma=\GammaOneHalf$).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Induction on $d$. If $\thick(K_{d-1})\ge\rho_{d-1}$ then $r_d=f\rho_{d-1}\le f\thick(K_{d-1})$, so $(\mathrm H_c)$ applies and $\thick(K_d)\ge\min(\thick(K_{d-1})-r_d,\ cr_d\sin(\pi/p))\ge\min(\rho_{d-1}-f\rho_{d-1},\ cf\rho_{d-1}\sin(\pi/p))=\gamma\rho_{d-1}=\rho_d$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:length} with $\tau\ge\rho_{d-1}$, $L(K_d)\le pL(K_{d-1})(1+f)+f\rho_{d-1}(2\pi q+\pi p)$; since $\rho_{d-1}\le\thick(K_{d-1})\le L(K_{d-1})/2\pi$, this is $\le L(K_{d-1})\bigl[p(1+f)+f(q+p/2)\bigr]=\mathcal A\,L(K_{d-1})$, and $L(K_0)=2\pi$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[on the constants]
$\Lambda_{\Rop}$ is generous: Lemma~\ref{lem:length} bounds $|K_d'|$ by the sum of the norms of two orthogonal vectors, uses $\kappa_{\max}\le1/\tau$ and $|\alpha|\le\pi$, and $(\mathrm H_c)$ with $c=1/2$ is twice weaker than the observed $\tau_d\approx r_d$. Numerically the growth factor per depth is $\GrowthHalfTwo$, $\GrowthHalfThree$ at $f=1/2$ (Table~\ref{tab:main}) against $\Lambda_{\Rop}=\LambdaHalf$; along the chain of the corollary itself ($r_d=f\rho_{d-1}$, \texttt{experiments/corollary\_chain.py}) the polygons give $L/\tau=\CorRopHalfOne$, $\CorRopHalfTwo$, $\CorRopHalfThree$ against $2\pi\Lambda_{\Rop}^d=\BoundHalfOne$, $\BoundHalfTwo$, $\BoundHalfThree$, and $\tau_d/\rho_d\ge\CorTauRhoMin$ for $f\in\{0.25,0.35,0.5\}$. We make no claim that any configuration in the family is stationary or optimal for ropelength, and none that the recursion is a ``flow law'' for tight recursive knots: it bounds one explicit construction.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[lower bounds, quoted]\label{rem:lower}\status{literature}
Buck and Simon \cite{BuckSimon1999} prove $\Rop(K)\ge c_{\rm BS}\,\Cr(K)^{3/4}$ for a universal $c_{\rm BS}>0$, and Cantarella, Kusner and Sullivan \cite{CKS2002} prove that ropelength minimisers exist in every knot and link type, are $C^{1,1}$, and satisfy further lower bounds. These need $\Cr(K_d)$. $K_2$ is a $2$-cable of the trefoil (Lemma~\ref{lem:frame}), which is alternating, hence adequate; Kalfagianni and McConkey \cite{KalfagianniMcConkey2024} prove that a cable with $q$ strands of an adequate knot $K$ has crossing number $\ge q^2\Cr(K)+1$, so $\Cr(K_2)\ge13$; their Corollary~1.2 gives the exact value $4\Cr(K)+1$ for the slopes $2w\pm1$, $w$ the writhe of a reduced alternating diagram, i.e.\ $\pm5,\pm7$ for the trefoil, and does not cover the slopes $9$ and $11$ of $K_2$, so we do not quote $\Cr(K_2)$. For $d\ge3$ only general satellite bounds are available, such as $\Cr\ge10^{-13}\Cr(\text{companion})$ \cite{Lackenby2014}. For $K_1=T(2,3)$ the best known numerical ropelength is about $32.7$ (numerical, uncertified; \cite{Pieranski1998,CKS2002}); our depth-one polygon at $f=1/2$ gives $\RopHalfOne$, i.e.\ the construction is not close to tight even at depth one.
\end{remark}

\begin{conjecture}[a threshold for $\thick(K_d)=r_d$]\label{conj}\status{conjectural}
For the default family there are thresholds $f_\ast(d)>0$ with $\inf_df_\ast(d)>0$ such that for $f\le f_\ast(d)$ every doubly critical pair of $K_d$ other than the antipodal pairs of one normal disc is at distance $>2r_d$ and $\minRad(K_d)>r_d$; hence $\thick(K_d)=r_d$ (the inequality $\le$ is Proposition~\ref{prop:upper}). Numerically the largest admissible $f_\ast(1)$ lies in $[0.35,0.5)$, and $f_\ast(2),f_\ast(3)\ge0.5$: by margins of only $\MarginHalfTwo\%$ and $\MarginHalfThree\%$ for the normalisation of Definition~\ref{def:family}, and of at least $\PhaseMarginTwoMin\%$ and $\PhaseMarginThreeMin\%$ over the normalisations tested (Section~\ref{sec:exp}).
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[growth rate]
If $\thick(K_d)=r_d$ for all large $d$ and $\minRad(K_{d-1})$ stays bounded below, then $\Rop_d/\Rop_{d-1}=(L_d/L_{d-1})(\tau_{d-1}/\tau_d)\to p/f$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:length}. The measured ratios ($\GrowthHalfTwo$, $\GrowthHalfThree$ at $f=1/2$ against $p/f=\PoverFHalf$; the other chains from Table~\ref{tab:main}) agree: the rate is a consequence of the conjecture, not an independent constant.
\end{remark}

\section{Limitations and next steps}

\emph{Limitations.} (1) The thickness lower bound ($p=2$, default family) is proved for all doubly critical pairs at every depth and completely at $d=1$; for $d\ge2$ the curvature of $K_d$ needs $(\mathrm H_3)$, whose constants are only measured on the polygons; their propagation in $d$ is partial (Corollary~\ref{cor:tolerance} for $d\ge3$ under a growth condition; Proposition~\ref{prop:noclose}), and $d=2$ is open. (2) The polygonal $\tau$ of \eqref{eq:tau} (vertex pairs, local-minimum test) is supported by $\tau_{pt}$, the segment check and stability in $N$, but is not certified. (3) Nothing is optimised; the family (pattern angle linear in $t$, $\varphi_d=0$, the frame normalisation of Definition~\ref{def:family}) is one of many, and the margins of Conjecture~\ref{conj} depend on the normalisation; for $p\ge3$ the thickness lower bound is treated only in Proposition~\ref{prop:partial}. (4) Only the thresholds $c_1\ge\frac12$, $c_0'\ge\frac12$, $1/(r\bar\kappa_1)>\KbarOneHalf$, the closed forms of Corollary~\ref{cor:samefamily} and the constants of Corollary~\ref{cor:tolerance} are analytic; every other constant of Section~\ref{sec:theory} is a grid infimum or supremum ($c_1$, $c_0'$ with a Lipschitz constant estimated on the grid itself, $\hat\kappa_d$ with a neighbour-increment correction, the v0.2 constant $c_0$ quoted for comparison on a $1500\times400$ grid without correction), with polygon-measured inputs for $d\ge2$; none uses interval arithmetic. (5) Writhe and holonomy are midpoint-rule sums, stable to $\WrDiffDefaultOne$ at depth one but only to $\WrDiffMax$ at depth three of $(3,2)$; the frame linking number for $f=0.5$ is decided by a margin of $\WrMarginHalf$; an exact polygonal writhe is not implemented. (6) The only lower bound specific to the family is Proposition~\ref{prop:upper}; knot-type bounds are limited by what is known of $\Cr(K_d)$ (Remark~\ref{rem:lower}).

\emph{Next steps.} (1) Make $(\mathrm H_3)$ uniform in $d$: by Proposition~\ref{prop:noclose} this needs control of all derivatives, e.g.\ an induction on analyticity in a complex strip $|\operatorname{Im}t|<\eta_d$ (the Bishop frame solves a linear ODE with analytic coefficients); with Corollary~\ref{cor:tolerance} this would prove $(\mathrm H_c)$ with $c=1/2$ for $(2,3)$ at every depth $d\ge3$, and Corollary~\ref{cor:rec} unconditionally once $d=2$ is settled. At $d=2$ the base $K_1$ is an explicit trigonometric polynomial, so \eqref{eq:kaprec} with $r_2\le r_1/2$ is a finite computation that interval arithmetic can certify. (2) Certify: interval arithmetic for $c_1$, $c_0'$, $\hat\kappa_d$ and for $\minRad$, $\dcsd$ of a piecewise-circular interpolant; exact polygonal writhe (Levitt; Klenin--Langowski). (3) A constrained length minimisation at fixed $\tau\ge1$ with a certified knot-type check, for $d=1,2$ (a curve-shortening probe of v0.1, \texttt{experiments/shrink.py}, gave nothing usable). (4) Optimise $f_d$ per depth and the pattern angle in arclength; treat $p\ge3$ (the bound $\delta_{R2}$ is general, the a priori bounds use $m\le2$ and $m_1=3/2$). (5) Match the conventions of \cite{KalfagianniMcConkey2024} with Lemma~\ref{lem:frame} to obtain $\Cr(K_2)$ and attach a Buck--Simon bound to each depth-two configuration.

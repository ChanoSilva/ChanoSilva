\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Research line EQO001 asked whether a formal relationship between equilibrium operators, variational inequalities and welfare criteria could yield results beyond the existing ones; its public record calls the formulation reviewed a combination of known components without an independent theorem. This note records that assessment. With equilibria the solutions of $\VI(F,K)$ on a closed convex set $K$ and welfare a $C^1$ functional on $K$, we prove the classical facts on which any such relationship rests: potentials, monotonicity, the equilibrium--welfare gap (Pigou, $4/3$) and the first-order coincidence whose repair is marginal-cost pricing. The one small addition concerns the cone $\Lam(x^*)$ of welfare weights for which a Nash equilibrium maximises $\sum_i\lambda_iu_i$: a closed convex cone inside a first-order cone $\Lone(x^*)$ built from $F(x^*)$, the normal cone and the marginal externalities, with equality under joint concavity; $(F,K)$ does not determine it. Exact examples show that $\Lam(x^*)$ need not be polyhedral (three players; three commodities with affine costs) and that $\Lam\ne\Lone$ for two commodities; deciding $\lambda\in\Lam(x^*)$ is co-NP-complete, even for strongly monotone quadratic games. Numerical checks on \RndTotalInst{} random monotone games confirm equality in every tested direction of every concave instance. No new equilibrium concept is offered.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

The variational-inequality formulation of equilibrium is one of the unifying ideas of applied mathematics: a Wardrop traffic equilibrium~\citep{wardrop1952}, a Nash equilibrium of a concave game~\citep{nash1951,rosen1965} and a Walrasian or spatial price equilibrium can all be written as: find $x^*$ in a closed convex set $K$ with $\ip{F(x^*)}{x-x^*}\ge 0$ for all $x\in K$~\citep{hartmanStampacchia1966,kinderlehrer1980,dafermos1980,harkerPang1990,nagurney1993,facchineiPang2003}. Welfare is an optimisation problem over the same $K$, and the temptation that motivated research line EQO001 is obvious: to look for a ``formal relationship'' between the inequality and the optimisation problem from which welfare statements about equilibria would follow.

The public record of the line is candid: the formulation reviewed combined known components without establishing an independent theorem, and the line was paused. This note discharges the line's objective in the only way that seems honest to us: by writing down, with complete proofs where they are short, what is a theorem in this area, what is a definition in disguise, and what remains open. We add one small result on the set of welfare weights that make a given equilibrium optimal; Remark~\ref{rem:novelty} states which parts of it are standard and which are, as far as we could check, only repackaged here.

\paragraph{Contributions and scope.} Section~\ref{sec:setting} fixes the setting; Section~\ref{sec:classical} proves the classical results; Section~\ref{sec:when} gives the first-order characterisation of welfare-optimal equilibria and marginal-cost pricing; Section~\ref{sec:cone} contains the welfare-weight cone (Theorem~\ref{thm:cone}), the impossibility statement (Proposition~\ref{prop:notdet}), five exact examples, three of them negative (Examples~\ref{ex:nonpoly},~\ref{ex:routing},~\ref{ex:three}), and the complexity of deciding membership (Proposition~\ref{prop:hard}); Sections~\ref{sec:exp}--\ref{sec:limits} report the numerical checks, what can be claimed, limitations and one open question. Everything is finite-dimensional and deterministic; every number in the text is produced by the scripts of Appendix~\ref{app:repro} with fixed seeds.

\section{Setting}\label{sec:setting}

Throughout, $K\subseteq\R^n$ is a nonempty closed convex set, $U\supseteq K$ an open set and $F\colon U\to\R^n$ a continuous map, the \emph{equilibrium operator}. We write $\ip{\cdot}{\cdot}$ for the Euclidean inner product.

\begin{definition}[Variational inequality, normal cone]\label{def:vi}
$\VI(F,K)$ is the problem of finding $x^*\in K$ such that $\ip{F(x^*)}{x-x^*}\ge 0$ for all $x\in K$; its solution set is $\SOL(F,K)$. The \emph{normal cone} of $K$ at $x\in K$ is $\NC_K(x)=\{v\in\R^n:\ip{v}{y-x}\le 0\ \forall y\in K\}$, a closed convex cone, equal to $\{0\}$ when $x$ is an interior point. By definition,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vi-normal}
x^*\in\SOL(F,K)\iff -F(x^*)\in\NC_K(x^*).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Monotonicity]
$F$ is \emph{monotone} on $K$ if $\ip{F(x)-F(y)}{x-y}\ge 0$ for all $x,y\in K$; \emph{strictly monotone} if the inequality is strict for $x\ne y$; \emph{$\mu$-strongly monotone} if $\ip{F(x)-F(y)}{x-y}\ge\mu\|x-y\|^2$ with $\mu>0$; and \emph{$L$-Lipschitz} if $\|F(x)-F(y)\|\le L\|x-y\|$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Game]\label{def:game}
A \emph{game} has $N$ players, strategy sets $K_i\subseteq\R^{n_i}$ closed and convex, $K=K_1\times\cdots\times K_N$, and payoffs $u_i\colon U\to\R$ of class $C^1$ on an open $U\supseteq K$, with $u_i(\cdot,x_{-i})$ concave on $K_i$ for every $x_{-i}$. A \emph{Nash equilibrium} is $x^*\in K$ with $u_i(x^*)\ge u_i(y_i,x^*_{-i})$ for all $i$ and $y_i\in K_i$. The \emph{pseudo-gradient} (equilibrium operator) is $F(x)=\big(-\nabla_{x_1}u_1(x),\dots,-\nabla_{x_N}u_N(x)\big)$, where $\nabla_{x_i}$ is the gradient with respect to player $i$'s own variables. The \emph{marginal externality} of player $j$ on player $i\ne j$ at $x$ is $\nabla_{x_j}u_i(x)\in\R^{n_j}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Nash equilibria are VI solutions]\label{lem:nash}
In the setting of Definition~\ref{def:game}, $x^*$ is a Nash equilibrium if and only if $x^*\in\SOL(F,K)$. \status{classical, \citealp{rosen1965}; \citealp{facchineiPang2003}}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a concave differentiable function $\phi$ on a convex set $C$, $z$ maximises $\phi$ over $C$ iff $\ip{\nabla\phi(z)}{y-z}\le 0$ for all $y\in C$: necessity because $t\mapsto\phi(z+t(y-z))$ is defined on $[0,1]$ and has right derivative $\ip{\nabla\phi(z)}{y-z}$ at $0$, which must be $\le 0$; sufficiency because $\phi(y)\le\phi(z)+\ip{\nabla\phi(z)}{y-z}$. Apply this to $\phi=u_i(\cdot,x^*_{-i})$ on $K_i$: $x^*$ is Nash iff $\ip{-F_i(x^*)}{y_i-x_i^*}\le 0$ for all $i$ and $y_i\in K_i$. Summing over $i$ gives $\ip{F(x^*)}{y-x^*}\ge 0$ for every $y\in K$; conversely, choosing $y=(y_i,x^*_{-i})$ in the VI recovers the $i$-th inequality.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Nonatomic routing]\label{def:wardrop}
A network has edges $E$, commodities $k=1,\dots,m$ with demands $d_k>0$ and path sets $P_k$, $P=\bigsqcup_k P_k$. A path flow $f\in\R^P_+$ is feasible if $\sum_{p\in P_k}f_p=d_k$ for each $k$; $K$ is the set of feasible flows, a product of scaled simplices. Edge flows are $f_e=\sum_{p\ni e}f_p$, edge costs $c_e\colon\R_+\to\R_+$ are continuous and nondecreasing, path costs are $c_p(f)=\sum_{e\in p}c_e(f_e)$, and the equilibrium operator is $F(f)=(c_p(f))_{p\in P}$. A \emph{Wardrop equilibrium} is a feasible $f^*$ such that, for every $k$ and $p,q\in P_k$ with $f^*_p>0$, $c_p(f^*)\le c_q(f^*)$. The \emph{social cost} is $C(f)=\sum_{p}f_pc_p(f)=\sum_e f_ec_e(f_e)$, and welfare is $W=-C$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Normal cone of a face of a simplex]\label{lem:simplex}
Let $\Delta=\{x\in\R^P_+:\sum_{p\in P_k}x_p=d_k,\ k=1,\dots,m\}$ and $x\in\Delta$ with support $S=\{p:x_p>0\}$. Then
\[
\NC_\Delta(x)=\{v: \exists\,\gamma\in\R^m \text{ with } v_p=\gamma_k \text{ for } p\in S\cap P_k,\ v_p\le\gamma_k \text{ for } p\in P_k\setminus S\}.
\]
\end{lemma}
This is the polar of the tangent cone $\{h:\sum_{p\in P_k}h_p=0\ \forall k,\ h_p\ge0\ (p\notin S)\}$, computed by Farkas' lemma~\citep{rockafellar1970}.

\begin{lemma}[Wardrop equilibria are VI solutions]\label{lem:wardrop}
$f^*$ is a Wardrop equilibrium iff $f^*\in\SOL(F,K)$ with $F=(c_p)_p$. \status{classical, \citealp{smith1979}; \citealp{dafermos1980}}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:vi-normal} and Lemma~\ref{lem:simplex}, $f^*\in\SOL(F,K)$ iff for each $k$ there is $\gamma_k$ with $c_p(f^*)=-\gamma_k$ on used paths of $P_k$ and $c_p(f^*)\ge-\gamma_k$ on unused ones, which is Wardrop's condition.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Welfare functionals]
A \emph{welfare functional} is a $C^1$ function $W\colon U\to\R$ to be maximised over $K$. For a game, the \emph{weighted welfare} with weights $\lambda\in\R^N_+$ is $W_\lambda=\sum_i\lambda_iu_i$; $\lambda=\mathbf 1$ is the utilitarian criterion. For routing, $W=-C$.
\end{definition}

\section{The classical results, with proofs}\label{sec:classical}

\subsection{Potentials}

A $C^1$ field $F$ with symmetric Jacobian on an open convex $U$ is a gradient (Poincar\'e's lemma): $P(x)=\int_0^1\ip{F(x_0+t(x-x_0))}{x-x_0}\,\dd t$ satisfies $\partial_iP(x)=\int_0^1\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\big[tF_i(x_0+t(x-x_0))\big]\dd t=F_i(x)$, by differentiation under the integral and $\partial_jF_i=\partial_iF_j$.

\begin{theorem}[Equilibria of gradient operators]\label{thm:potential}
Suppose $F=\nabla P$ on $U\supseteq K$ with $P\in C^1(U)$. Then
\[
\SOL(F,K)=\{x\in K:\ip{\nabla P(x)}{y-x}\ge 0\ \forall y\in K\},
\]
the set of stationary points of $P$ over $K$; it contains $\arg\min_KP$, and equals it when $P$ is convex on $K$. If $P$ is strictly convex, $\SOL(F,K)$ has at most one point. \status{classical: \citealp{beckmann1956} for routing, \citealp{mondererShapley1996} for potential games; proved here}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first identity is Definition~\ref{def:vi} with $F=\nabla P$. If $x$ minimises $P$ over $K$ then, as in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:nash}, the right derivative of $t\mapsto P(x+t(y-x))$ at $0$ is nonnegative, which is the stationarity inequality. If $P$ is convex and $x$ is stationary, $P(y)\ge P(x)+\ip{\nabla P(x)}{y-x}\ge P(x)$ for all $y\in K$. If $P$ is strictly convex its minimiser over the convex set $K$ is unique.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The three classical instances]\label{rem:instances}
(a) \emph{Routing}~\citep{beckmann1956}. For continuous $c_e$, $P(f)=\sum_e\int_0^{f_e}c_e(s)\,\dd s$ is $C^1$ with $\partial P/\partial f_p=\sum_{e\in p}c_e(f_e)=c_p(f)$ (fundamental theorem of calculus), convex when the $c_e$ are nondecreasing; Wardrop equilibria minimise $P$, with unique edge flows when all $c_e$ are strictly increasing. $P\ne C$: the potential integrates the cost, the social cost multiplies it (Section~\ref{sec:classical}.3).
(b) \emph{Concave games}~\citep{rosen1965}. Lemma~\ref{lem:nash} and Theorem~\ref{thm:mono}(a) give uniqueness under Rosen's diagonal strict concavity, which for unit weights is strict monotonicity of the pseudo-gradient.
(c) \emph{Potential games}~\citep{mondererShapley1996}. A $C^1$ game has exact potential $\Pi$ iff $F=-\nabla\Pi$; its equilibria are the stationary points of $-\Pi$ over $K$. The potential is bookkeeping, not welfare (Example~\ref{ex:cournot}(i)).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Monotonicity: uniqueness, existence and a solver}

\begin{lemma}[Projection]\label{lem:proj}
Let $\Pi_K$ be the Euclidean projection onto $K$. Then $z=\Pi_K(y)$ iff $z\in K$ and $\ip{y-z}{x-z}\le 0$ for all $x\in K$; $\Pi_K$ is nonexpansive; and for every $\gamma>0$, $x\in\SOL(F,K)$ iff $x=\Pi_K(x-\gamma F(x))$. \status{classical, \citealp[Chapter~I]{kinderlehrer1980}}
\end{lemma}
\begin{theorem}[Monotone operators]\label{thm:mono}
(a) If $F$ is strictly monotone on $K$, $\SOL(F,K)$ has at most one point. (b) If $F$ is $\mu$-strongly monotone and $L$-Lipschitz on $K$, $\SOL(F,K)$ is a singleton $\{x^*\}$, and for $0<\gamma<2\mu/L^2$ the iteration $x_{t+1}=\Pi_K(x_t-\gamma F(x_t))$ satisfies $\|x_{t+1}-x^*\|\le q\|x_t-x^*\|$ with $q=\sqrt{1-2\gamma\mu+\gamma^2L^2}<1$; for $\gamma=\mu/L^2$, $q=\sqrt{1-\mu^2/L^2}$. \status{classical, \citealp{kinderlehrer1980,facchineiPang2003}; proved here}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(a) If $x,y\in\SOL(F,K)$ then $\ip{F(x)}{y-x}\ge 0$ and $\ip{F(y)}{x-y}\ge 0$; adding, $\ip{F(x)-F(y)}{x-y}\le 0$, so $x=y$ by strict monotonicity. (b) Let $T(x)=\Pi_K(x-\gamma F(x))$. By nonexpansiveness,
$\|Tx-Ty\|^2\le\|(x-y)-\gamma(F(x)-F(y))\|^2=\|x-y\|^2-2\gamma\ip{F(x)-F(y)}{x-y}+\gamma^2\|F(x)-F(y)\|^2\le q^2\|x-y\|^2$,
and $q<1$ iff $\gamma<2\mu/L^2$. Banach's fixed-point theorem on the complete set $K$ gives a unique fixed point, which is the unique VI solution by Lemma~\ref{lem:proj}, and the contraction estimate is the stated rate.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:hs}
For merely continuous $F$ on compact convex $K$, existence is the Hartman--Stampacchia theorem (\citealp[Lemma~3.1]{hartmanStampacchia1966}), obtained from Brouwer's theorem applied to $T$; we do not reproduce it. All solvers in Section~\ref{sec:exp} are the iteration of Theorem~\ref{thm:mono}(b) with $\gamma=\mu/L^2$, so each computed equilibrium comes with a uniqueness proof and a residual certificate; the exception is Example~\ref{ex:nonpoly} (E2c), where $F$ is constant, $\gamma=1$ is used and uniqueness follows from dominance.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Equilibrium is not welfare: Pigou and the price of anarchy}

\begin{example}[Pigou]\label{ex:pigou}
Two parallel links, unit demand, $c_1\equiv 1$ and $c_2(x)=x^d$, $d\ge 1$~\citep{pigou1920}. Every user on link 2 pays $1\le c_1$, so $f^*=(0,1)$ is a Wardrop equilibrium with $C(f^*)=1$, and it is the only one: a feasible flow with $f_1>0$ has $f_2<1$, so its users on link 1 pay $1>f_2^d$. The social cost of $(1-x,x)$ is $1-x+x^{d+1}$, minimised at $x=(d+1)^{-1/d}$ with value $1-\tfrac{d}{d+1}(d+1)^{-1/d}$. For $d=1$ the optimum is $(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ with cost $\tfrac34$ and ratio $\tfrac43$; the ratio tends to infinity with $d$, since $(d+1)^{-1/d}\to 1$. The computed values are in \path{results/tables_traffic.md}.
\end{example}

\begin{theorem}[Price of anarchy for affine costs]\label{thm:poa}
If every $c_e(x)=a_ex+b_e$ with $a_e,b_e\ge 0$, then for every Wardrop equilibrium $f$ and every feasible $f'$, $C(f)\le\tfrac43C(f')$. The constant is attained in Example~\ref{ex:pigou} with $d=1$. \status{classical, \citealp{roughgardenTardos2002}, see also \citealp{roughgarden2005}; the proof below follows \citealp{correa2004}}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:wardrop}, $\sum_pc_p(f)(f'_p-f_p)\ge 0$; regrouping by edges, $C(f)=\sum_ec_e(f_e)f_e\le\sum_ec_e(f_e)f'_e$. For each edge,
$c_e(f_e)f'_e=c_e(f'_e)f'_e+a_e(f_e-f'_e)f'_e\le c_e(f'_e)f'_e+\tfrac14a_ef_e^2\le c_e(f'_e)f'_e+\tfrac14c_e(f_e)f_e$,
since $y\mapsto a_e(f_e-y)y$ is maximised at $y=f_e/2$ and $a_ef_e^2\le(a_ef_e+b_e)f_e$. Summing, $C(f)\le C(f')+\tfrac14C(f)$.
\end{proof}

\section{When an equilibrium is a welfare maximiser}\label{sec:when}

\begin{proposition}[Necessary and sufficient first-order conditions]\label{prop:nec}
Let $x^*\in\SOL(F,K)$ and let $W$ be differentiable at $x^*$.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,label=(\alph*)]
\item If $x^*\in\arg\max_KW$ then $\nabla W(x^*)\in\NC_K(x^*)$ and $-F(x^*)\in\NC_K(x^*)$. If $W$ is concave on $K$, the converse holds: $x^*\in\arg\max_KW$ iff $\nabla W(x^*)\in\NC_K(x^*)$.
\item If $x^*$ is an interior point of $K$, (a) forces $F(x^*)=0=\nabla W(x^*)$. If $\NC_K(x^*)=\R_+\nu$ is a ray and $F(x^*)\ne 0$, (a) forces $\nabla W(x^*)=-\theta F(x^*)$ for some $\theta\ge 0$.
\item (Simplices.) If $K$ is a product of simplices (Lemma~\ref{lem:simplex}), both $F(x^*)$ and $\nabla W(x^*)$ are constant on the support of $x^*$ within each commodity (hence parallel there when both are non-zero), and each satisfies Wardrop's principle with respect to its own coordinates. On $K=\{x\ge0:Ax=b\}$ the same holds with ``constant on the support'' replaced by ``equal on the support to an element of the row space of $A$''.
\item If $F$ is strictly monotone, $W$ is strictly concave and both $\SOL(F,K)$ and $\arg\max_KW$ are nonempty, then $\SOL(F,K)=\arg\max_KW$ iff the unique equilibrium satisfies $\nabla W(x^*)\in\NC_K(x^*)$; otherwise the two sets are disjoint.
\end{enumerate}
\status{proved here; elementary}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a) The first-order necessary condition for a maximum of a differentiable function over a convex set (proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:nash}) is $\ip{\nabla W(x^*)}{y-x^*}\le 0$ for all $y\in K$, i.e. $\nabla W(x^*)\in\NC_K(x^*)$; the second membership is \eqref{eq:vi-normal}; sufficiency under concavity is the same argument as in Theorem~\ref{thm:potential}. (b) For interior $x^*$, $\NC_K(x^*)=\{0\}$. If $\NC_K(x^*)=\R_+\nu$, write $-F(x^*)=\alpha\nu$ and $\nabla W(x^*)=\beta\nu$ with $\alpha,\beta\ge 0$; $F(x^*)\ne 0$ gives $\alpha>0$ and $\theta=\beta/\alpha$. (c) Lemma~\ref{lem:simplex} applied to $-F(x^*)$ and to $\nabla W(x^*)$; for $\{x\ge0:Ax=b\}$ the polar of the tangent cone is $\{A^{\!\top}w-v:v\ge0,\ v_S=0\}$ by Farkas' lemma. (d) Both sets are singletons by Theorem~\ref{thm:mono}(a) and strict concavity; apply (a).
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:nec} is a statement about first-order conditions and nothing more: it says that an equilibrium can be a welfare optimum only if the two problems happen to share their KKT point, and it gives no mechanism producing that coincidence. The one mechanism that always works is to change the operator.

\begin{corollary}[Marginal-cost pricing and the toll set]\label{cor:toll}
Let $C\in C^1(U)$ be a cost to be minimised over $K$ and define the \emph{toll field} $\tau=\nabla C-F$. Then
\[
\SOL(F+\tau,K)=\{x\in K:-\nabla C(x)\in\NC_K(x)\}\supseteq\arg\min_KC,
\]
with equality when $C$ is convex on $K$, and at most one point when $C$ is strictly convex; the tolled operator is a gradient field with potential $C$. For separable routing costs with $c_e\in C^1$, $\tau_p(f)=\sum_{e\in p}f_ec_e'(f_e)$, the Pigou--Knight--Beckmann marginal-cost toll. More generally, for a target $\bar x\in K$ the set of constant toll vectors $t\in\R^n$ with $\bar x\in\SOL(F+t,K)$ is the translated cone $-F(\bar x)-\NC_K(\bar x)$, polyhedral when $K$ is; choosing in it the toll closest to a reference vector is an inverse optimisation problem in the sense of \citet{ahujaOrlin2001}, and for routing it is the toll-set description of \citet{hearnRamana1998}. \status{classical, \citealp{pigou1920,knight1924,beckmann1956,hearnRamana1998}; proved here}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
$F+\tau=\nabla C$, so the first statement is Theorem~\ref{thm:potential} with $P=C$. For routing, $\partial C/\partial f_p=\sum_{e\in p}\big(c_e(f_e)+f_ec_e'(f_e)\big)$, whence $\tau_p$. Finally $\bar x\in\SOL(F+t,K)$ iff $-F(\bar x)-t\in\NC_K(\bar x)$ by \eqref{eq:vi-normal}.
\end{proof}

\section{The cone of welfare weights}\label{sec:cone}

Fix a game as in Definition~\ref{def:game} and a Nash equilibrium $x^*$. Write $u=(u_1,\dots,u_N)\colon K\to\R^N$ and $G(x^*)=Du(x^*)\in\R^{N\times n}$ for the Jacobian of the payoff vector, whose $i$-th row is $\nabla u_i(x^*)^{\!\top}$ (all variables, own and others'). Define
\[
\Lam(x^*)=\{\lambda\in\R^N_+: x^*\in\arg\max_KW_\lambda\},\qquad
\Lone(x^*)=\{\lambda\in\R^N_+: G(x^*)^{\!\top}\lambda\in\NC_K(x^*)\}.
\]
For a set $D\subseteq\R^N$, $D^\circ=\{\lambda:\ip{\lambda}{d}\le 0\ \forall d\in D\}$ is its polar cone.

\begin{theorem}[Welfare-weight cone]\label{thm:cone}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,label=(\alph*)]
\item $\Lam(x^*)=\R^N_+\cap\{u(x)-u(x^*):x\in K\}^\circ$. In particular $\Lam(x^*)$ is a closed convex cone.
\item $\Lam(x^*)\subseteq\Lone(x^*)$, and $\Lone(x^*)$ is a closed convex cone, polyhedral whenever $K$ is polyhedral.
\item If every $u_i$ is concave on $K$ (jointly in all variables), then $\Lam(x^*)=\Lone(x^*)$. More generally, equality holds whenever $W_\lambda$ is concave on $K$ for every $\lambda\in\Lone(x^*)$.
\item (Block form.) $\lambda\in\Lone(x^*)$ iff for every player $j$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block}
-\lambda_jF_j(x^*)+\sum_{i\ne j}\lambda_i\nabla_{x_j}u_i(x^*)\in\NC_{K_j}(x_j^*).
\end{equation}
Since $-F_j(x^*)\in\NC_{K_j}(x_j^*)$ already, a sufficient condition is that the $\lambda$-weighted externality $\sum_{i\ne j}\lambda_i\nabla_{x_j}u_i(x^*)$ lies in $\NC_{K_j}(x_j^*)$ for every $j$; when $x_j^*$ is interior to $K_j$, \eqref{eq:block} says exactly that this weighted externality vanishes.
\item $\Lam(x^*)\ne\{0\}$ implies that $x^*$ is weakly Pareto efficient in $K$ (no $x\in K$ with $u_i(x)>u_i(x^*)$ for all $i$); under the concavity of (c), the converse holds. Without any concavity, if $x^*$ is locally weakly Pareto efficient then $\Lone(x^*)\ne\{0\}$.
\end{enumerate}
\status{proved here; (a) and (e) are the standard scalarisation argument of multi-objective optimisation \citep{geoffrion1968,ehrgott2005}, and $\Lam(x^*)$ is the cell at $u(x^*)$ of the weight-set decomposition studied for linear problems by \citet{bensonSun2000}}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(a) $\lambda\in\Lam(x^*)$ iff $\lambda\ge 0$ and $\ip{\lambda}{u(x)-u(x^*)}\le 0$ for all $x\in K$, which is the stated intersection; polar cones and the orthant are closed convex cones.
(b) If $x^*$ maximises the differentiable $W_\lambda$ over $K$, the first-order condition gives $\nabla W_\lambda(x^*)=\sum_i\lambda_i\nabla u_i(x^*)=G(x^*)^{\!\top}\lambda\in\NC_K(x^*)$. $\Lone(x^*)$ is the preimage of the closed convex cone $\NC_K(x^*)$ under a linear map, intersected with $\R^N_+$; when $K$ is polyhedral so is $\NC_K(x^*)$, hence so is $\Lone(x^*)$.
(c) For $\lambda\ge 0$, $W_\lambda$ is concave when the $u_i$ are, and the first-order condition is then sufficient (Proposition~\ref{prop:nec}(a)); the general statement is the same argument.
(d) For a product set, $v\in\NC_K(x^*)$ iff $v_j\in\NC_{K_j}(x_j^*)$ for each $j$: taking $y\in K$ that differs from $x^*$ in block $j$ only shows necessity, and summing the block inequalities shows sufficiency. Block $j$ of $G(x^*)^{\!\top}\lambda$ is $\sum_i\lambda_i\nabla_{x_j}u_i(x^*)=\lambda_j\nabla_{x_j}u_j(x^*)+\sum_{i\ne j}\lambda_i\nabla_{x_j}u_i(x^*)$ and $\nabla_{x_j}u_j(x^*)=-F_j(x^*)$. The sufficient condition follows because $\NC_{K_j}(x_j^*)$ is a convex cone containing $-F_j(x^*)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:nash}; for interior $x_j^*$, $\NC_{K_j}(x_j^*)=\{0\}$ forces $F_j(x^*)=0$ and then \eqref{eq:block} reads $\sum_{i\ne j}\lambda_i\nabla_{x_j}u_i(x^*)=0$.
(e) If $\lambda\in\Lam(x^*)\setminus\{0\}$ and $u(x)>u(x^*)$ componentwise, then $\ip{\lambda}{u(x)}>\ip{\lambda}{u(x^*)}$, a contradiction. Conversely, under concavity the set $A=u(K)-\R^N_+$ is convex; weak efficiency means $u(x^*)+\varepsilon\mathbf 1\notin A$ for every $\varepsilon>0$, so $u(x^*)$ is a boundary point of $A$ and a supporting hyperplane gives $\lambda\ne 0$ with $\ip{\lambda}{a}\le\ip{\lambda}{u(x^*)}$ for all $a\in A$; since $u(x^*)-\R^N_+\subseteq A$, $\lambda\ge 0$, and $a=u(x)$ gives $\lambda\in\Lam(x^*)$. Finally, if $\Lone(x^*)=\{0\}$, the convex cone $\{sG(x^*)(x-x^*):x\in K,\ s\ge0\}$ meets the open orthant: otherwise a separating hyperplane (the Gordan--Motzkin alternative) gives $\lambda\ge0$, $\lambda\ne0$, with $\ip{\lambda}{G(x^*)(x-x^*)}\le0$ on $K$, i.e.\ $\lambda\in\Lone(x^*)$; and $G(x^*)(x-x^*)>0$ gives $u(x^*+\tau(x-x^*))>u(x^*)$ for small $\tau>0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What is standard and what is not]\label{rem:novelty}
The scalarisation in (a) and (e) is textbook, and the set of weights under which a given point maximises the weighted sum is a standard object of multi-objective optimisation (the weight-set decomposition, polyhedral in the linear case \citep{bensonSun2000}); Proposition~\ref{prop:notdet} repackages the Nikaid\^o--Isoda gap function and is an instance of best-response equivalence \citep{morrisUi2004}. What we did not find in the literature we consulted, and claim only as packaging, is the reading of \eqref{eq:block}: the cone is cut out by the equilibrium face (through $\NC_{K_j}(x_j^*)$ and $F_j(x^*)$, both determined by $(F,K)$) and by the marginal externalities $\nabla_{x_j}u_i(x^*)$, which are \emph{not} determined by $(F,K)$. The last claim of (e) and the interior case of (d) give the first-order mechanism behind the theorem of \citet{dubey1986} that, for interior equilibria and generically in the payoffs, Nash equilibria of smooth games are Pareto-inefficient: at an interior $x^*$, local weak efficiency forces some $\lambda\ge0$, $\lambda\ne0$, to annihilate every weighted externality; Example~\ref{ex:cournot}(i) is an instance.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[$(F,K)$ does not determine welfare]\label{prop:notdet}
Let the $u_i$ be continuous on $K=\prod K_i$ with $K_i$ compact, and let $x^*$ be a Nash equilibrium. Define $v_i(x_{-i})=\max_{y_i\in K_i}u_i(y_i,x_{-i})$ and $u_i'=u_i-v_i$. Then the game $(u_i')$ has the same best-response correspondences and the same Nash equilibria as $(u_i)$; wherever $\nabla_{x_i}u_i$ exists, $\nabla_{x_i}u_i'=\nabla_{x_i}u_i$, so the two games have the same equilibrium operator; and $x^*$ maximises every $W'_\lambda=\sum_i\lambda_iu_i'$, $\lambda\in\R^N_+$, over $K$: $\Lam'(x^*)=\R^N_+$. Consequently welfare optimality of an equilibrium is not a property of $(F,K)$: every pair $(F,K)$ that arises from a game is compatible with $\Lam(x^*)=\R^N_+$; if $N\ge2$ and some $K_{-i}$ is not a singleton, it is also compatible with $e_i\notin\Lam(x^*)$ (and the pair of Example~\ref{ex:cournot}(i) with $\Lam(x^*)=\{0\}$). \status{proved here; $\sum_i(v_i-u_i)$ is the Nikaid\^o--Isoda gap function~\citep{nikaidoIsoda1955}, and $u\mapsto u'$ a best-response equivalence~\citep{morrisUi2004}}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$v_i$ does not depend on $x_i$, so subtracting it changes neither $\arg\max_{y_i}u_i(y_i,x_{-i})$ nor $\nabla_{x_i}u_i$. By construction $u_i'\le 0$ on $K$, and $u_i'(x^*)=0$ because $x_i^*$ is a best response to $x^*_{-i}$; hence $W'_\lambda\le 0=W'_\lambda(x^*)$ for every $\lambda\ge 0$. For the second claim, add to $u_i$ the term $c\ip{d}{x_{-i}-x^*_{-i}}$, with $d$ such that $\delta=\ip{d}{x_{-i}-x^*_{-i}}>0$ for some $x_{-i}\in K_{-i}$: neither $F$ nor the equilibria change, the game stays in the class of Definition~\ref{def:game}, and $x=(x_i^*,x_{-i})$ beats $x^*$ for $W_{e_i}$ once $c\delta>u_i(x^*)-u_i(x)$.
\end{proof}

The modified game need not be $C^1$, so $\Lam'(x^*)$ is understood in the exact sense of Theorem~\ref{thm:cone}(a). When best responses are unique, Danskin's theorem~\citep{danskin1966} gives $v_i\in C^1$ with $\nabla v_i(x_{-i})=\nabla_{x_{-i}}u_i(b_i(x_{-i}),x_{-i})$; the modified game is then in the class of Definition~\ref{def:game} and has \emph{zero marginal externalities at $x^*$}, so $\Lone'(x^*)=\R^N_+$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:cone}(d), the first-order reason for the proposition.

\begin{example}[Two players, exact cone]\label{ex:wedge}
Let $K=[0,1]^2$, $\alpha>1$, $\beta_1,\beta_2\ge 0$, and
\[
u_1(x)=\alpha x_1-\tfrac12x_1^2-\beta_1x_2,\qquad u_2(x)=\alpha x_2-\tfrac12x_2^2-\beta_2x_1 .
\]
Each player's action harms the other linearly. The equilibrium operator is $F(x)=(x_1-\alpha,x_2-\alpha)$, with Jacobian $I$, so $F$ is $1$-strongly monotone and $1$-Lipschitz and does not depend on $\beta$; the unique equilibrium is $x^*=(1,1)$, at which $\NC_K(x^*)=\R^2_+$ and $F(x^*)=(1-\alpha)\mathbf 1$. Here $G(x^*)=\begin{psmallmatrix}\alpha-1&-\beta_1\\-\beta_2&\alpha-1\end{psmallmatrix}$, so by \eqref{eq:block}
\[
\Lone(x^*)=\{\lambda\ge 0:\ (\alpha-1)\lambda_1\ge\beta_2\lambda_2,\ (\alpha-1)\lambda_2\ge\beta_1\lambda_1\}.
\]
Both payoffs are jointly concave (Hessians $-\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$ and $-\operatorname{diag}(0,1)$), so $\Lam(x^*)=\Lone(x^*)$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:cone}(c). Thus the cone is a wedge, non-trivial iff $\beta_1\beta_2\le(\alpha-1)^2$ and two-dimensional iff the inequality is strict; for $\alpha=2$, $\beta_1=\beta_2=\tfrac12$ it is $\{\tfrac12\lambda_2\le\lambda_1\le2\lambda_2\}$ (Figure~\ref{fig:wedge}): the corner equilibrium is welfare-optimal exactly when neither player is weighted more than twice the other. Games with different $\beta$ have the same $F$ and different cones: Proposition~\ref{prop:notdet} in miniature.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Cournot duopoly]\label{ex:cournot}
Let $u_i(q)=q_i(a-q_1-q_2)$ on $K=[0,1]^2$; $\partial^2u_1/\partial q_1^2=-2<0$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:nash} applies, and $F(q)=(2q_1+q_2-a,\ q_1+2q_2-a)$ has symmetric part $\begin{psmallmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{psmallmatrix}\succ0$. (i) For $a=1$ the equilibrium $q^*=(\tfrac13,\tfrac13)$ is interior, $F(q^*)=0$, and $\partial u_1/\partial q_2=-q_1^*\ne 0$: by Theorem~\ref{thm:cone}(d) the interior condition fails for every $\lambda\ne 0$, so $\Lam(q^*)=\{0\}$ (Dubey's inefficiency, Remark~\ref{rem:novelty}). (collusion at $(\tfrac14,\tfrac14)$ gives each firm $\tfrac18>\tfrac19$). The game has the exact potential $\Pi(q)=a(q_1+q_2)-q_1^2-q_2^2-q_1q_2$, maximised at the equilibrium: a potential is not a welfare functional. (ii) For $a=4$ the equilibrium is the corner $(1,1)$, $F(q^*)=(-1,-1)$, $G(q^*)=\begin{psmallmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{psmallmatrix}$ and $\Lone(q^*)=\{\lambda_1=\lambda_2\ge 0\}$, a ray. The payoffs are not jointly concave, so Theorem~\ref{thm:cone}(c) is silent; but $W_{(1,1)}(q)=(q_1+q_2)(4-q_1-q_2)$ is concave, so $\Lam(q^*)=\Lone(q^*)$ nevertheless. Concavity is sufficient, not necessary.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Three players: a non-polyhedral cone]\label{ex:nonpoly}
Let $K=[0,1]^3$ and
\[
u_1=x_1,\qquad u_2=x_2,\qquad u_3=x_3-\tfrac12x_1^2+\tfrac14x_1+\tfrac12x_1x_2-\tfrac12x_2 .
\]
Each payoff is linear, hence concave, in the player's own variable, so the game is in the class of Definition~\ref{def:game}; $F\equiv(-1,-1,-1)$, $x^*=(1,1,1)$ is the unique Nash equilibrium and $\NC_K(x^*)=\R^3_+$. Since $\nabla u_3(x^*)=(-\tfrac14,0,1)$, $G(x^*)^{\!\top}\lambda=(\lambda_1-\tfrac14\lambda_3,\lambda_2,\lambda_3)$ and $\Lone(x^*)=\{\lambda\ge 0:\lambda_1\ge\tfrac14\lambda_3\}$. We compute $\Lam(x^*)$ exactly. On $\{\lambda_3=0\}$, $W_\lambda=\lambda_1x_1+\lambda_2x_2$ is maximised at $x^*$, so $\R^2_+\times\{0\}\subseteq\Lam(x^*)$; as $\Lam(x^*)$ is a cone it suffices to fix $\lambda_3=1$. Then $W_\lambda$ is increasing in $x_3$, so $x_3=1$, and affine in $x_2$, so the maximum over $x_2$ is attained at $x_2\in\{0,1\}$. For $x_2=1$, $W_\lambda=\tfrac12+\lambda_2+(\lambda_1+\tfrac34)x_1-\tfrac12x_1^2$ is maximised on $[0,1]$ at $x_1=1$ iff $\lambda_1\ge\tfrac14$, with value $W_\lambda(x^*)=\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\tfrac34$. For $x_2=0$, $W_\lambda=1+(\lambda_1+\tfrac14)x_1-\tfrac12x_1^2$ has maximum $1+\tfrac12(\lambda_1+\tfrac14)^2$ if $\lambda_1\le\tfrac34$ (at $x_1=\lambda_1+\tfrac14$) and $\lambda_1+\tfrac34\le W_\lambda(x^*)$ otherwise. Hence $\lambda\in\Lam(x^*)$ iff $\lambda_1\ge\tfrac14$ and $\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\tfrac34\ge1+\tfrac12(\lambda_1+\tfrac14)^2$, i.e.
\[
\Lam(x^*)\cap\{\lambda_3=1\}=\big\{\lambda_1\ge\tfrac14,\ \lambda_2\ge\tfrac12\big(\tfrac34-\lambda_1\big)_+^2\big\}.
\]
Its boundary contains the parabolic arc $\lambda_2=\tfrac12(\tfrac34-\lambda_1)^2$, $\tfrac14\le\lambda_1\le\tfrac34$, which no finite union of segments contains (a line meets a parabola in at most two points); since the slice of a polyhedral cone by a hyperplane is a polyhedron, $\Lam(x^*)$ \emph{is not polyhedral}, and $\Lam(x^*)\subsetneq\Lone(x^*)$ with a curved boundary. For instance $\lambda=(0.3,0,1)\in\Lone\setminus\Lam$: the point $(0.55,0,1)$ improves $W_\lambda$ by $0.10125$. The degeneracy $F\equiv$ const is inessential. Subtract $\tfrac\varepsilon2x_i^2$ from each $u_i$, $0<\varepsilon<1$: $F(x)=\varepsilon x-\mathbf 1$ is $\varepsilon$-strongly monotone, $x^*$ is unchanged, $\Lone\cap\{\lambda_3=1\}=\{\lambda_1\ge\frac1{4(1-\varepsilon)}\}$, and for $\frac1{4(1-\varepsilon)}\le\lambda_1\le\frac3{4(1-\varepsilon)}$ a point of the slice $\lambda_3=1$ lies in $\Lam(x^*)$ iff
\[
\lambda_2\ge\varphi(\lambda_1)=\frac{(\lambda_1+\frac14)^2/(2(1+\varepsilon\lambda_1))+\frac14}{1-\varepsilon/2}-\lambda_1,\qquad \varphi''(\lambda_1)=\frac{(1-\varepsilon/4)^2}{(1-\varepsilon/2)(1+\varepsilon\lambda_1)^3}>0,
\]
which reduces to the parabola for $\varepsilon=0$; so $\Lam(x^*)$ is not polyhedral for a strongly monotone $F$ either. Indeed $x_3=1$ as before; on the edge $x_2=0$ the maximum, at $x_1=\frac{\lambda_1+1/4}{1+\varepsilon\lambda_1}\le1$, is $1-\frac\varepsilon2+\frac{(\lambda_1+1/4)^2}{2(1+\varepsilon\lambda_1)}$, which exceeds $W_\lambda(x^*)=(1+\lambda_1+\lambda_2)(1-\frac\varepsilon2)-\frac14$ iff $\lambda_2<\varphi(\lambda_1)$. Conversely let $\lambda_2\ge\varphi(\lambda_1)$. If the Hessian of $W_\lambda$ in $(x_1,x_2)$ is negative semidefinite, $\lambda\in\Lone$ suffices; otherwise it is indefinite (its $(1,1)$ entry is $-(1+\varepsilon\lambda_1)<0$), the maximum lies on an edge, and on the edges $x_1=1$, $x_2=1$ and $x_1=0$ the value is at most $W_\lambda(x^*)$ (increasing in $x_2$; increasing in $x_1$ because $\lambda_1\ge\frac1{4(1-\varepsilon)}$; at most $(1+\lambda_2)(1-\frac\varepsilon2)<W_\lambda(x^*)$). \status{proved here; the counterexample was pointed out in the internal review of v0.1, the closed form for $\varepsilon>0$ in that of v0.2}
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Two commodities: $\Lam\ne\Lone$ in routing]\label{ex:routing}
Theorem~\ref{thm:cone}(a),(b) use neither the Nash property nor the product structure: they hold for any finite family of $C^1$ functions $u_k$ on $K$ and any $x^*\in K$, with $\Lam$ and $\Lone$ defined by the same formulas. Apply them to the costs $C_k(f)=\sum_{p\in P_k}f_pc_p(f)$ borne by the commodities of a network, $u_k=-C_k$, at a Wardrop equilibrium $f^*$. Take two commodities with unit demands: commodity 1 chooses between a private edge of cost $f+2$ and a shared edge of cost $f$; commodity 2 between a private edge of cost $f/4$ and the same shared edge. With $y=(f_B,f_D)\in[0,1]^2$ the flows sent to the shared edge (path flows are determined by edge flows here, so $f^*$ is a well-defined point),
\[
C_1(y)=(1-f_B)(3-f_B)+f_B(f_B+f_D),\qquad C_2(y)=\tfrac14(1-f_D)^2+f_D(f_B+f_D),
\]
both with indefinite Hessians (determinant $-1$, from the shared-edge cross term), so (c) is unavailable. The Wardrop equilibrium is $f^*=(1,0)$ (shared path cost $1\le 2$ for commodity 1, private cost $\tfrac14\le 1$ for commodity 2), unique because the reduced operator $y\mapsto(c_B-c_A,\,c_D-c_C)$ has Jacobian $\begin{psmallmatrix}2&1\\1&5/4\end{psmallmatrix}\succ 0$; $C(f^*)=(1,\tfrac14)$. At $f^*$, in the reduced coordinates $y$ (the cones $\Lam$, $\Lone$ are invariant under this affine reparametrisation of $K$), $\NC(f^*)=\R_+\times\R_-$, $\nabla C_1(f^*)=(0,1)$ and $\nabla C_2(f^*)=(0,\tfrac12)$, so $-\lambda_1\nabla C_1-\lambda_2\nabla C_2=(0,-\lambda_1-\tfrac12\lambda_2)\in\NC_K(f^*)$ for every $\lambda\ge 0$: $\Lone(f^*)=\R^2_+$. For $\Lam$, fix $\lambda_2=1$ ($\lambda_2=0$ gives $\lambda\in\Lam$: $C_1$ is minimised at $f^*$). The Hessian of $\lambda_1C_1+C_2$ is $\begin{psmallmatrix}4\lambda_1&\lambda_1+1\\\lambda_1+1&5/2\end{psmallmatrix}$, positive semidefinite iff $10\lambda_1\ge(\lambda_1+1)^2$, in which case the first-order condition suffices and $\lambda\in\Lam$. Otherwise the minimum over the box lies on an edge, where the restriction is a convex quadratic: on $f_B=1$ and on $f_D=0$ it is attained at $f^*$ (derivatives $\lambda_1+\tfrac12+\tfrac52t>0$ and $4\lambda_1(f_B-1)\le 0$); on $f_D=1$ the value is at least $\tfrac{15}{8}\lambda_1+1>\lambda_1+\tfrac14$; on $f_B=0$ it is $3\lambda_1+\tfrac14(1-t)^2+t^2$, minimised at $t=\tfrac15$ with value $3\lambda_1+\tfrac15$. Hence $f^*$ is optimal iff $\lambda_1+\tfrac14\le3\lambda_1+\tfrac15$, and
\[
\Lam(f^*)=\{\lambda\ge 0:\lambda_2\le 40\lambda_1\}\subsetneq\Lone(f^*)=\R^2_+ .
\]
For $\lambda=(0,1)$, the flow $y=(0,\tfrac15)$ lowers $C_2$ from $\tfrac14$ to $\tfrac15$ (while $C_1$ rises from $1$ to $3$): $f^*$ is a corner at which the KKT conditions hold for every weighting, while the per-commodity costs are non-convex. ($f^*$ is also the utilitarian optimum.) \status{proved here; verified in E5}
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Three commodities: a non-polyhedral commodity cone]\label{ex:three}
Three commodities with unit demand; commodity $k$ chooses between a private edge and one shared edge of cost $f$; the private costs are $f/4$, $f/4$ and the constant $2$. With $y\in[0,1]^3$ the flows on the shared edge and $S=y_1+y_2+y_3$,
\[
C_k(y)=\tfrac14(1-y_k)^2+y_kS\quad(k=1,2),\qquad C_3(y)=2(1-y_3)+y_3S .
\]
The Wardrop equilibrium is $y^*=(0,0,1)$ (shared cost $1\le2$ for commodity 3, private cost $\tfrac14\le1$ for commodities 1, 2), unique because the reduced operator $y\mapsto(S-\tfrac14(1-y_1),S-\tfrac14(1-y_2),S-2)$ has Jacobian $\operatorname{diag}(\tfrac14,\tfrac14,0)+\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\!\top}\succ0$. At $y^*$, in reduced coordinates, $\NC=\R_-\times\R_-\times\R_+$, $\nabla C_1=(\tfrac12,0,0)$, $\nabla C_2=(0,\tfrac12,0)$ and $\nabla C_3=(1,1,0)$, so $\Lone(y^*)=\R^3_+$. We claim
\[
\Lam(y^*)=\{\lambda\ge0:\lambda_3+m(\lambda_1,\lambda_2)\ge0\},\qquad m=\min_{[0,1]^2}P,
\]
$P(u,w)=\tfrac{\lambda_1}4(u^2-2u)+\tfrac{\lambda_2}4(w^2-2w)+(u+w)(\lambda_1u+\lambda_2w)$.
Write $y=(u,w,t)$, $\Phi_\lambda=\sum_k\lambda_kC_k$ and $c=(\lambda_1+\lambda_3)u+(\lambda_2+\lambda_3)w\ge0$; expanding, $\Phi_\lambda(y)-\Phi_\lambda(y^*)=P(u,w)+\lambda_3+t(c-2\lambda_3)+\lambda_3t^2$. For fixed $(u,w)$ this is convex in $t$. If $c\ge2\lambda_3$ its minimum over $t\in[0,1]$ is at $t=0$ and equals $P+\lambda_3\ge m+\lambda_3$. Otherwise it is attained at $t=1-c/(2\lambda_3)$ and equals $P+c-c^2/(4\lambda_3)\ge P+\tfrac c2\ge P+\tfrac12(\lambda_1u+\lambda_2w)\ge\tfrac14(\lambda_1u^2+\lambda_2w^2)\ge0$. Hence $y^*$ is a minimiser iff $\lambda_3+m\ge0$, and when $\lambda_3+m<0$ the minimiser of $P$ on the face $t=0$ beats it. On the slice $\lambda_2=1$, for $\tfrac23<\lambda_1<\tfrac32$, $P$ is strictly convex with minimiser $\big(3\lambda_1-2,\ \lambda_1(3-2\lambda_1)\big)/\big((4-\lambda_1)(4\lambda_1-1)\big)$ in the open square, and $m=-\lambda_1(\lambda_1+1)/\big(4(4-\lambda_1)(4\lambda_1-1)\big)$. So the boundary of $\Lam(y^*)\cap\{\lambda_2=1\}$ over that interval is the graph of the rational, non-affine function $\psi(\lambda_1)=\lambda_1(\lambda_1+1)/\big(4(4-\lambda_1)(4\lambda_1-1)\big)$: $\Lam(y^*)$ \emph{is not polyhedral}, and $\Lam\subsetneq\Lone=\R^3_+$. For instance $\psi(1)=\tfrac1{18}$, and for $\lambda=(1,1,\tfrac1{20})$ the flow $y=(\tfrac19,\tfrac19,0)$ lowers $\Phi_\lambda$ by $\tfrac1{180}$. \status{proved here; the three-commodity expectation of v0.2 was confirmed numerically in the internal review, round 2; verified in E7}
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[Deciding membership is co-NP-complete]\label{prop:hard}
For games with scalar actions in $[0,1]$ and quadratic payoffs with rational coefficients, given with a Nash equilibrium $x^*$ and a rational $\lambda\ge0$, deciding whether $\lambda\in\Lam(x^*)$ is co-NP-complete, and so is deciding whether $\Lam(x^*)=\Lone(x^*)$. Both remain co-NP-hard when $F$ is strongly monotone, $x^*$ is a vertex of $K$, $\lambda$ is a unit vector and $\Lone(x^*)=\R^N_+$. \status{proved here; pointed out in the internal review, round 2; checked by brute force in E6}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Membership in co-NP.} $\lambda\notin\Lam(x^*)$ iff $W_\lambda(x)>W_\lambda(x^*)$ for some $x\in K$. Let $\hat x$ maximise $W_\lambda$ over $K$ and let $\Phi$ be the face of $K$ in whose relative interior it lies. The restriction of the quadratic $W_\lambda$ to the affine hull of $\Phi$ has a local, hence global, maximum at $\hat x$; it is therefore concave there, and every point of the nonempty polytope $\{x\in\Phi:\nabla_\Phi W_\lambda(x)=0\}$ (gradient in the free coordinates) is a maximiser. A vertex of this polytope has encoding size polynomial in the input \citep[Ch.~10]{schrijver1986} and is the certificate, checked in exact arithmetic (for quadratic programming over general polyhedra this is the theorem of \citet{vavasis1990}; deciding even \emph{local} optimality in an indefinite quadratic programme is NP-hard \citep{murtyKabadi1987}). For $\Lam\ne\Lone$, a certificate is an extreme-ray generator $\lambda$ of the polyhedral cone $\Lone(x^*)$, of polynomial size, together with such an $x$ for it: if every generator lay in the convex cone $\Lam$, then $\Lone\subseteq\Lam$.
\emph{Hardness.} We reduce from MAX-CUT \citep{karp1972}: given a graph with nonnegative integer weights $w_{ij}$ on $n$ nodes and an integer $k\ge1$, is there a cut of weight $\ge k$? Let $\mathrm{cut}(x)=\sum_{ij}w_{ij}(x_i+x_j-2x_ix_j)$, $L(x)=\sum_i\deg_w(i)x_i\ge\mathrm{cut}(x)$ on $[0,1]^n$, and $q(x,y)=\mathrm{cut}(x)+y(k-\tfrac12-L(x))$. As $q$ is affine in each variable separately, its maximum over $[0,1]^{n+1}$ is attained at a vertex, where $q=\mathrm{cut}(x)$ if $y=0$ and $q=\mathrm{cut}(x)-L(x)+k-\tfrac12\le k-\tfrac12=q(0,1)$ if $y=1$; so $(0,1)$ maximises $q$ iff every cut has weight $<k$. Take players with actions $x_1,\dots,x_n,y,z\in[0,1]$, fix $0<\varepsilon<1$, and let $u_i=-x_i-\tfrac\varepsilon2x_i^2$, $u_{n+1}=y-\tfrac\varepsilon2y^2$, $u_{n+2}=z-\tfrac\varepsilon2z^2+q(x,y)$. Each payoff is concave in the own action, $F(v)=\varepsilon v+(\mathbf 1_n,-1,-1)$ is $\varepsilon$-strongly monotone, and $x^*=(0,\dots,0,1,1)$ is the unique equilibrium (strictly dominant strategies). $W_{e_{n+2}}=u_{n+2}$ separates in $z$, so $e_{n+2}\in\Lam(x^*)$ iff every cut has weight $<k$. At $x^*$, $\nabla_xq=0$ and $\partial_yq=k-\tfrac12>0$, so $\Lone(x^*)=\R^{n+2}_+$ by \eqref{eq:block}; the other unit vectors lie in $\Lam(x^*)$ by dominance, so $\Lam(x^*)=\Lone(x^*)$ iff $e_{n+2}\in\Lam(x^*)$. The construction is polynomial. (Merging the first $n+1$ players into one player with a vector action gives the same conclusions with $N=2$.)
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What remains of Question~8.1 of v0.2]\label{rem:q1}
For polynomial payoffs on a polyhedral $K$, $\Lam(x^*)$ is semialgebraic (Tarski--Seidenberg, \citealp{bochnakCosteRoy1998}), polyhedral for $N=2$ (as is every closed convex cone of $\R^2$) and not in general for $N\ge3$ (Example~\ref{ex:nonpoly}). By Proposition~\ref{prop:hard}, unless P $=$ NP there is no polynomial-time test of $\lambda\in\Lam(x^*)$ or of $\Lam=\Lone$, hence no polynomial-time computable description of $\Lam(x^*)$ by polynomial inequalities. We withdraw the request of v0.2 for a characterisation ``in terms of the active-face pattern and the Hessians'': in that generality it has no efficiently checkable answer. Tractable subclasses (scalar actions with $N$ fixed; supermodular $W_\lambda$, as in Example~\ref{ex:nonpoly}, where polyhedrality still fails and the cut-based reduction does not apply) are directions, not questions we pose.
\end{remark}

\section{What can be claimed, limitations and open question}\label{sec:claims}\label{sec:limits}

Every statement carries its status in line; the claim-by-claim table of v0.2 is now \path{results/CLAIMS.md}. In three lines: the classical results are theorems, proved above; the ``formal relationship'' between equilibrium and welfare is the first-order coincidence of Proposition~\ref{prop:nec}, a definition rather than a mechanism; and $(F,K)$ carries no welfare information (Proposition~\ref{prop:notdet}), so welfare statements need the externalities as separate data, and even with them deciding whether a weighting makes an equilibrium optimal is co-NP-complete (Proposition~\ref{prop:hard}).

\paragraph{Limitations.}
(1) Everything is finite-dimensional, deterministic and $C^1$; mixed strategies, atomic congestion games, dynamics and stochastic demand are outside the note. (2) Theorem~\ref{thm:cone} is a first-order statement; its only general sufficient condition for equality is concavity, which excludes Cournot-type payoffs and multi-commodity routing costs. (3) The experiments verify theorems on quadratic games over boxes and on small networks; they are exact only because $N\le4$ (E2--E5, E7) or $n\le6$ (E6), and the reported frequencies describe one sampling distribution. (4) We have not surveyed the toll-pricing and multi-objective literature exhaustively (Remark~\ref{rem:novelty}).

\begin{question}[Complexity for routing]\label{q:multi}
In a multi-commodity network with affine costs let $C_k(f)=\sum_{p\in P_k}f_pc_p(f)$ and let $f^*$ be a Wardrop equilibrium \emph{in path flows} ($\Lam(f^*)$ depends on the representative when path flows are not unique). Theorem~\ref{thm:cone}(a),(b) give $\Lam(f^*)\subseteq\Lone(f^*)$. When no edge is shared the problem separates by commodity, each $C_k$ is convex in the flows of commodity $k$, and $\Lam=\Lone$ is the face of the orthant on which $\lambda_k>0$ only if $f^{*k}$ minimises $C_k$; otherwise $\Lam\ne\Lone$ already for two commodities (Example~\ref{ex:routing}; E5 also with every $b_e=0$), and $\Lam$ need not be polyhedral for three (Example~\ref{ex:three}). Membership $\lambda\in\Lam(f^*)$ is in co-NP by the argument of Proposition~\ref{prop:hard}. Is it co-NP-hard when the network and the number of commodities are part of the input? Proposition~\ref{prop:hard} does not apply, because the $C_k$ have the special form above; a reduction or a polynomial algorithm would settle the question.
\end{question}

\paragraph{Next steps.} Settle Question~\ref{q:multi}; run E4 and E5 with a global solver for larger $N$ and $m$; and confirm with the author whether the ``formulation reviewed'' in the line's record was an instance of Proposition~\ref{prop:nec} or of Corollary~\ref{cor:toll}, which is our reading.

\paragraph{Exact tests and random families.} For each of the $3^N$ patterns in $\{0,1,\text{free}\}^N$ the free coordinates solve the stationarity system of $W_\lambda$ restricted to that face; the best feasible candidate is the maximum over the box, because a maximiser lies in the relative interior of some face and is stationary for the restriction (singular systems are handled in \path{welfare_cone.py}). $\lambda\in\Lam$ iff $W_\lambda(x^*)\ge\max_KW_\lambda-10^{-8}$; a \emph{certified witness} improves $W_\lambda$ by more than $10^{-6}$.
